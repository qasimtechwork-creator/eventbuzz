Black Friday Deals Tips: How to Find Real Discounts in 2026

DRAFT — not published yet · Prepared October 12, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Every November, retailers compete to make their discounts look the biggest. Some cuts are genuine; some are theater — a price raised in October so it can be "slashed" in November. This guide shows how to tell the difference and find real Black Friday discounts in 2026, so the money you save is money actually saved.

Quick answer: Track the prices of items you want from early November, compare every deal across at least two stores, stack coupons and cashback on top of sale prices, and ignore the size of the claimed discount — only the price you pay compared to recent weeks matters.

Set Your Price Baseline Before November

The most common Black Friday trick is the inflated reference price: an item priced higher in October so the November "discount" looks dramatic. Your defense is a baseline. In early November, write down the current selling price of each item on your list — not the crossed-out "was" price, the actual price you would pay today. When Black Friday arrives, compare against your own numbers. A genuine deal beats the price the item actually sold for in recent weeks, not a theoretical list price nobody paid.

Price-history tools and browser extensions can automate this for popular products. Even a simple notes list on your phone works: item, store, price, date. Ten minutes of note-taking in early November can save real money on the 27th.

Compare Every Deal Across Stores

Never buy from the first store that shows you a discount. Check at least two or three retailers for the same model — model numbers matter, because some "exclusive" Black Friday versions of TVs and appliances are slightly different builds with different specs. Compare the exact model, the delivered price (including shipping), and the return policy. Many large retailers also run price-match guarantees through the holidays; if you buy early and the price drops later, a price-match or price-adjustment policy can refund the difference — but read the current terms first, because these policies change from year to year.

Stack Coupons, Cashback and Loyalty Rewards

The advertised sale price is often just the starting point. Extra layers stack on top:

  • Store coupons and promo codes: Check the retailer's coupon page and newsletter signup offers before checkout; some apply automatically, some need a code.
  • Cashback portals and cards: Cashback rates frequently rise during Black Friday week. Compare the portal rate against your credit card's rewards — you usually cannot stack both on the same purchase, so pick the richer one.
  • Loyalty programs: Free loyalty accounts at major chains unlock member-only prices and early access windows. Signing up is free and takes minutes.
  • Gift card deals: Some stores discount their own gift cards during the season; buying discounted gift cards first, then spending them on sale items, compounds the saving.

Add each layer to your price comparison. A smaller headline discount with stacked coupons and cashback can beat a bigger advertised cut with nothing on top.

Spot the Classic Black Friday Traps

  • The fake "was" price: A discount percentage means nothing if the reference price was never real. Trust your baseline, not the percentage.
  • Doorbuster limits: Extreme deals on a handful of units exist mainly to get you through the door — or onto the site. Decide in advance whether the item is actually on your list.
  • Urgency theater: Countdown timers and "only 3 left" badges are designed to rush you. If you did your research, a few minutes of calm comparison costs nothing.
  • Warranty and accessory upsells: Extended warranties and overpriced cables at checkout can quietly erase the discount you just earned. Decline, then research separately if you truly want them.
  • Buying what you would not otherwise buy: The deepest discount in the store is worthless on something you did not need. The list you made in November is the boss.

Time Your Purchases: Early, Friday or Monday?

Not everything should wait for November 27. Items with steady prices and limited stock — popular toys, specific sizes in apparel — can sell out before Black Friday, so buying at a fair early-November price beats missing out entirely. Big-ticket electronics and TVs reward patience: the deepest cuts in those categories typically land during Black Friday week itself, with online-leaning categories like computers often peaking on Cyber Monday, November 30. For smaller gifts, the final days of Cyber Week often bring the widest simultaneous discounts. Match the timing to the category, and to how badly you need the specific item.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a Black Friday deal is real?

Compare the sale price against what the item actually sold for in recent weeks — your own early-November baseline or a price-history tool. If the "deal" price matches September's regular price, it is marketing, not a discount.

Should I buy early or wait for Black Friday?

Wait for big-ticket electronics and TVs, where the deepest cuts land during Black Friday week. Buy early if the item has limited stock (popular toys, specific apparel sizes) and the current price is already fair.

Do online Black Friday prices match in-store prices?

Usually yes at the same retailer, but not always — some doorbusters are in-store only and some online codes do not work at the register. Check both channels for the items that matter most.

Are doorbusters worth chasing?

Only if the item is already on your list. Doorbusters are limited-quantity offers designed to draw crowds; chasing one for something you had not planned to buy is how savings turn into spending.

What is the best way to track prices?

A simple written baseline in early November works: item, store, price, date. Price-history browser tools automate it for popular products. Either way, the point is comparing against reality, not against the claimed "was" price.

Conclusion

Real Black Friday savings come from preparation, not from luck or speed. Baseline your prices, compare across stores, stack every layer of savings, and buy only what is on your list. Do that, and November 27 becomes the cheapest day of your year instead of the most expensive.
