Ramadan 2027 Dates: When Does Ramadan Start, Calendar & Key Nights

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Families start planning for Ramadan months ahead, arranging leave, menus, charity and nights of prayer around one gentle month. For 2027 the expected dates are set out below in one simple calendar, so you can plan with confidence while remembering that the Islamic calendar moves with the moon. Quick answer: Ramadan is expected to begin on Monday, February 8, 2027 in most countries, with Pakistan expected to begin on Tuesday, February 9, 2027. Final dates are confirmed by the sighting of the moon, so please confirm locally before fixing travel or leave.

Why the Dates Are Expected, Not Final

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic Hijri calendar, which follows the lunar cycle. A new month begins when the crescent moon is sighted after sunset, usually announced by national moon sighting committees and local mosques. Because sighting depends on weather, location and local practice, neighbouring countries can begin a day apart. Every date on this page is therefore marked as expected, and final dates are confirmed by the sighting of the moon.

Ramadan 2027 Calendar Overview

Based on the expected start, the month would run as follows. If the moon is sighted a day earlier or later in your country, every date below shifts by that same day.

  • Expected first roza: Monday, February 8, 2027 in most countries; Tuesday, February 9, 2027 in Pakistan.
  • Expected Fridays in Ramadan: February 12, February 19, February 26 and March 5, 2027.
  • Expected last ten nights begin: Around February 27 to 28, 2027, depending on your start date.
  • Expected 27th night window: Approximately March 6 to 7, 2027, moon sighting dependent.
  • Expected end of Ramadan: Around March 8 to 9, 2027, followed by Eid al-Fitr.

Key Nights to Prepare For

Every night of Ramadan carries reward, but the last ten nights hold a special place because Laylatul Qadr, the Night of Power, is sought among them. Many families plan lighter work, extra charity and family prayer time for this final stretch. Our Laylatul Qadr guide explains the signs, duas and what to do on those nights.

  • Odd nights to seek Laylatul Qadr: The 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th and 29th nights, expected from late February to around March 8, 2027.
  • Most widely observed: The 27th night, expected around March 6 to 7, 2027, though the exact night is not known for certain.
  • Taraweeh prayers: Begin the night before the first fast and continue through the month at most mosques.

How to Use This Calendar

Use the expected dates to request leave, plan community iftars and prepare your home, then confirm the final start with your local announcement on the evening before. Frequently Asked Questions

When does Ramadan start in 2027?

Ramadan is expected to begin on Monday, February 8, 2027 in most countries, and on Tuesday, February 9, 2027 in Pakistan. Final dates are confirmed by the sighting of the moon.

When does Ramadan end in 2027?

Ramadan is expected to end around March 8 to 9, 2027, after 29 or 30 days of fasting, with Eid al-Fitr following the sighting of the Shawwal moon.

What is the expected date of Laylatul Qadr in 2027?

Laylatul Qadr is sought on the odd nights of the last ten nights. The 27th night, the most widely observed, is expected around March 6 to 7, 2027, but it can fall on any odd night, so the whole final stretch matters.

Why can Ramadan start on different days in different countries?

The Hijri month begins with the sighting of the crescent moon, and visibility varies by location and weather. Some countries follow local sighting and others follow regional announcements, which can create a one day difference.

How many days is Ramadan 2027?

Like every Ramadan, it will be 29 or 30 days depending on when the next crescent moon is sighted at the end of the month.

Conclusion

Mark Monday, February 8, 2027 in pencil, keep March 6 to 7 free for the 27th night, and let the moon make the final announcement. A little early planning turns Ramadan from a rush into the peaceful month it is meant to be.
