What Is Advent? The Season of Waiting, Explained Simply

By EventBuzz Team • October 21, 2026 • 8 min read

What is Advent? Put simply, it is the four week season of waiting and preparation that leads Christians to Christmas, a deliberate slowdown built into the calendar centuries before December became the busiest month of the year. Advent begins on the fourth Sunday before Christmas Day, which in 2026 is Sunday, November 29, and runs until Christmas Eve. This explainer covers what the season means, where its customs come from, what the wreath and its candles signify, and simple ways any household can observe it without adding one more obligation to a full month.

For the countdown tools families pair with Advent, see our guides to Advent calendars and countdown ideas, and for the readings that accompany the candles, our Christmas Bible verses and Christmas prayers. We begin with the word itself, because the name tells the whole story.

The Meaning of the Word and the Season

Advent comes from the Latin word adventus, meaning coming or arrival. The season looks in two directions at once: back to the first coming of Jesus, whose birth Christmas celebrates, and forward in hope. Churches have marked a preparation period before Christmas for well over a thousand years in various forms, with the four Sunday structure settling into place in the early medieval church. The tone is distinct from Christmas itself: where Christmas is feasting and arrival, Advent is anticipation, more candlelight than celebration, a held breath before the good news.

That difference is the season’s gift to modern households. Advent gives December a shape other than shopping and school concerts: four Sundays, four small thresholds, each with a theme to think about and a candle to light. Families who observe even a light version often report that Christmas Day lands differently, less like the end of an exhausting relay and more like a destination they actually walked toward, one week at a time.

When Advent Falls in 2026

Advent always begins on the Sunday nearest to November 30th, the feast of St. Andrew, which means it lasts between three and four weeks depending on the year. In 2026, the four Sundays of Advent fall on November 29, December 6, December 13 and December 20, with Christmas Day itself arriving on Friday, December 25. That is a comfortably full Advent of nearly four weeks, and the final candle is lit on December 20, leaving the last run up to Christmas Eve for final preparations.

The season that Advent prepares for does not end on December 25th either: Christmas itself traditionally runs for twelve days, from December 25th to January 5th, closing with Epiphany on January 6th. Our guide to the 12 days of Christmas explains that often misunderstood stretch, and our Three Kings Day guide covers its joyful finale. Advent is the doorway; knowing where it leads makes the waiting feel purposeful rather than long.

The Advent Wreath and Its Four Candles

The wreath is Advent’s most loved symbol: a circle of evergreen, unbroken like the love it represents, holding four candles lit one by one across the Sundays. In the most common Western pattern, three candles are purple, the season’s color of preparation and royalty, and one is rose or pink, lit on the third Sunday, traditionally called Gaudete Sunday from the Latin for rejoice, a bright midpoint in a reflective season. A fifth white candle, the Christ candle, is sometimes placed in the center and lit on Christmas Eve or Christmas Day.

The wreath as a household custom is widely traced to nineteenth century Germany, where a pastor named Johann Hinrich Wichern is credited with a large wheel of candles to help children count the days, later simplified into the four candle wreath churches and homes adopted. Candle themes vary by tradition and country: hope, peace, joy and love is a beloved modern pattern, while others remember the prophets, Bethlehem, the shepherds and the angels. There is no single enforced scheme, which is part of the custom’s charm; your wreath can carry the meanings your household needs most this year.

The Four Weeks: Hope, Peace, Joy and Love

Week one, hope: the season opens with the prophets and with longing, the sense that the story begins with people waiting for light in a dark time. Week two, peace: the focus turns to preparing the way, often through John the Baptist’s call and the journey toward Bethlehem. Week three, joy: the rose candle Sunday, when the shepherds enter the story and the mood lifts, rejoicing is the assignment. Week four, love: the final days belong to Mary and Joseph, the angel’s announcements, and love arriving in person.

Hold the themes loosely and they become practical. Hope week might mean writing one honest hope for the year ahead. Peace week might mean resolving one small family friction or clearing one chaotic corner. Joy week is permission, in the middle of the busiest fortnight, to do something purely delightful. Love week lends itself to the season’s most generous acts, the visit, the call, the gift that costs time instead of money. Themes turn four Sundays from a countdown into a curriculum of the heart, for children and adults alike.

Advent Calendars and Other Customs

The Advent calendar grew from German Protestant customs of marking the days, chalk lines, candles burned down a notch daily, until printed calendars with little doors appeared in the early twentieth century and conquered the world. Today calendars hold chocolate, pictures, verses, toys, or acts of kindness, and our Advent calendars guide compares the styles. The religious cousins of the calendar include the Jesse Tree, which tells the Bible’s story through one ornament and reading a day, and daily devotions that walk through the prophecies and the nativity accounts week by week.

Music has its own Advent register too: hymns like O Come, O Come, Emmanuel belong to the waiting weeks, while many churches save the full blaze of carols for Christmas itself. If your household sings, our Christmas carol lyrics guide will carry you from the first Sunday through the last verse of the season. The pattern underneath every custom is identical: small, daily, cumulative. Advent spirituality is built from teaspoons, not buckets.

Simple Ways to Observe Advent at Home

Start smaller than your ambitions. A wreath or four candles on the table, lit each Sunday with one verse and one minute of quiet, is a complete observance, and our verses collection and prayers collection supply the words. Add one kindness practice, a reverse calendar that gives an item away daily fills many homes with the season’s real spirit, and one act of deliberate slowness a week: a walk, a story read aloud, an evening with the tree lights and no screens. That is Advent, faithfully kept, in about an hour a week.

With children, let them own the visible parts: lighting the candle with supervision, opening the calendar, placing the Jesse Tree ornament, choosing whose name goes in the week’s prayer. Children remember rituals they helped perform far longer than lectures they heard, and the questions they ask at the wreath, why is that one pink, why do we wait, are Advent doing its oldest work. However lightly or deeply your household keeps it, the season’s promise is the same: waiting, done together and on purpose, turns out to be a celebration of its own.

Conclusion

Advent is Christmas’s wise older sibling: four weeks that teach the heart to arrive before the day does. It begins in 2026 on Sunday, November 29, lights its candles one by one through hope, peace, joy and love, and hands you over to Christmas Eve ready rather than ragged. You need almost nothing to keep it, four candles, a few verses, one small kindness at a time, and the willingness to wait on purpose. Start this year, start small, and let the waiting itself become one of the gifts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Advent in simple terms?

Advent is the four week Christian season of waiting and preparation before Christmas. Beginning on the fourth Sunday before Christmas Day, it focuses on anticipation rather than celebration, traditionally marked with a wreath of four candles lit one per Sunday, with themes like hope, peace, joy and love.

When does Advent start in 2026?

Advent 2026 begins on Sunday, November 29. The four Sundays fall on November 29, December 6, December 13 and December 20, and Christmas Day arrives on Friday, December 25. The pink or rose candle is traditionally lit on the third Sunday, December 13.

What do the four Advent candles mean?

In the most common pattern the candles carry the themes of hope, peace, joy and love, though traditions vary: some name them for the prophets, Bethlehem, the shepherds and the angels. Three are usually purple and one, for the third joyful Sunday, is rose or pink; a white Christ candle may be added at Christmas.

What is the difference between Advent and Christmas?

Advent is the preparation, Christmas is the celebration. Advent’s mood is anticipation, reflection and candlelight across the four weeks before December 25th. The Christmas season itself traditionally runs twelve days, from December 25th through January 5th, ending with Epiphany on January 6th.

How can a busy family observe Advent simply?

Light a wreath or four candles each Sunday with one short verse or prayer, keep one daily practice like a calendar or a reverse giving box, and take one slow family moment a week. An hour a week, spread across small rituals, captures the season without adding pressure to a full December.
