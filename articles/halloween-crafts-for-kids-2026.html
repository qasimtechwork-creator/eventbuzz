Easy Halloween Crafts for Kids: 25 Fun DIY Projects 2026

By EventBuzz Team • October 14, 2026 • 8 min read

Halloween crafts keep kids happily busy all October — and the decorations they make become treasured keepsakes. These 25 easy Halloween crafts for kids in 2026 are organized by age group, use affordable supplies, and range from 10-minute quickies to weekend projects.

Crafts for Toddlers (Ages 2-4)

  • Handprint ghosts: White handprints on black paper with drawn faces. Simple, adorable, frameable.
  • Cotton ball ghosts: Glue cotton balls onto ghost shapes. Great sensory activity.
  • Pumpkin stamping: Halved apples dipped in orange paint make perfect pumpkin prints.
  • Sticker scenes: Halloween sticker sheets on colored paper — zero mess, endless creativity.
  • Tissue paper pumpkins: Scrunch orange tissue into balls, add a green pipe-cleaner stem.

Crafts for Preschoolers (Ages 4-6)

  • Paper plate masks: Decorate plates as pumpkins, cats, or monsters; add elastic straps.
  • Footprint ghosts: White footprints on dark paper become a ghost family — add faces and names.
  • Egg carton bats: Paint egg carton cups black, add wings and googly eyes.
  • Popsicle stick scarecrows: Glue sticks into figures, add fabric scraps and drawn faces.
  • Leaf ghosts: Collect large leaves, paint white, draw faces. Nature meets Halloween.

Crafts for School-Age Kids (Ages 7-12)

  • Mason jar lanterns: Paint jars with Halloween scenes; add LED tea lights. Beautiful at night.
  • Origami bats: Fold black paper bats to hang from doorways. Impressive and inexpensive.
  • Yarn-wrapped pumpkins: Wrap foam pumpkins in orange yarn for textured, modern decor.
  • Haunted house dioramas: Shoebox scenes with construction paper, cotton webs, and tiny figures.
  • Slime — Halloween edition: Orange and black slime with plastic spiders mixed in. The ultimate kid craft.
  • Perler bead designs: Melted bead art in pumpkin, ghost, and bat shapes.

Group and Classroom Crafts

  • Collaborative mural: A long paper roll where each child adds to a Halloween scene.
  • Class pumpkin patch: Each student decorates a paper pumpkin for a hallway display.
  • Ghost garland: An assembly line of tissue ghosts strung together for classroom decoration.
  • Monster bookmarks: Practical, personal, and useful long after Halloween.

Weekend Project Crafts

  • Cardboard haunted house: A big box becomes a multi-room haunted house with cut windows and painted details.
  • Paper mache pumpkins: Balloons, newspaper strips, and paste — messy, memorable, and fantastic results.
  • Sock puppets: Old socks become vampires, witches, and ghosts with felt and googly eyes.
  • Halloween wreath: A foam wreath base decorated with kid-chosen Halloween picks.
  • Costume accessories: Make masks, wands, or crowns to wear on the big night.

Essential Craft Supplies

  • Construction paper in Halloween colors, googly eyes in bulk, washable paints and markers.
  • Glue sticks (less mess than liquid glue), kid-safe scissors, paper plates and cups.
  • Pipe cleaners, cotton balls, tissue paper, and paper bags for puppets.
  • A dedicated "craft smock" or old shirt per child saves clothing.

Tips for Crafting with Kids

  • Prep materials in advance — pre-cut tricky shapes for younger kids.
  • Cover tables with disposable cloths for fast cleanup.
  • Let kids lead creatively; "wrong" colors and silly faces are the point.
  • Display finished crafts prominently — a gallery wall or clothesline showcase.
  • Photograph the process, not just the product — the messy hands are the memory.

Organizing a Halloween Craft Party

Turn crafting into the party itself:

  • Setup: 3-4 craft stations around the room, each with a different project and difficulty level. Kids rotate freely.
  • Stations idea: Pumpkin decorating (no carve), mask making, slime station, and cookie decorating.
  • Timing: 2 hours maximum for ages 5-8; up to 3 hours for older kids.
  • Take-home: Provide bags or boxes for transporting wet or fragile crafts.
  • Adults: One adult per station keeps things smooth. Recruit early.

Cost control: A craft party for 10 kids costs $30-$50 using dollar-store and bulk supplies. Ask each family to contribute one supply item to spread the cost. The crafts double as party favors — no separate favor budget needed.

Display and preserve: Kids' Halloween crafts deserve better than the recycling bin. Create a dedicated October gallery wall, photograph each creation for a digital album, and let kids choose one favorite to preserve in a keepsake box each year. Watching the collection grow — from wobbly toddler handprints to intricate older-kid designs — becomes its own Halloween tradition that the whole family treasures.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are easy Halloween crafts for toddlers?

Handprint ghosts, cotton ball ghosts, apple-stamp pumpkins, and sticker scenes are perfect for toddlers. They are simple, sensory-rich, and require minimal precision — ideal for ages 2-4.

What craft supplies do I need for Halloween kids' crafts?

Stock construction paper, googly eyes, washable paints, glue sticks, kid-safe scissors, paper plates, pipe cleaners, and cotton balls. These basics cover most Halloween projects.

How do I manage mess during kids' Halloween crafts?

Cover tables with disposable cloths, use washable supplies, dress kids in smocks, prep materials in advance, and keep wet wipes handy. Embrace some mess — it is part of the fun.
