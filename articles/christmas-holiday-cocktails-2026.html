Holiday Cocktails & Mocktails 2026: Festive Drink Recipes

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 5 min read

Every great holiday gathering has a signature drink. Whether it is a slow-simmered mulled wine warming the kitchen or a sparkling cranberry mocktail the kids can raise in a toast, festive drinks set the tone for the whole evening. The best holiday drinks share a few things: seasonal flavors like cranberry, cinnamon, citrus and pomegranate, beautiful presentation, and recipes simple enough to make while guests are arriving. Here are the standouts for 2026.

Signature Holiday Cocktails

Mulled wine is the undisputed classic: gently heat red wine with orange slices, cinnamon sticks, cloves and a little honey, keeping it warm but never boiling. Homemade eggnog with freshly grated nutmeg beats anything from a carton, and a splash of rum or bourbon makes it a proper holiday treat. For something lighter, a cranberry vodka spritz with lime and soda is refreshing between rich courses. Serve cocktails in warmed glasses when the drink is hot; it keeps them at temperature longer and feels noticeably more luxurious.

Festive Mocktails for Everyone

Good mocktails are not an afterthought. A sparkling apple-cranberry punch with rosemary sprigs looks stunning in a pitcher and takes minutes to assemble. Hot spiced apple cider with cinnamon and orange is the cold-weather answer to everything. For kids, a candy-cane hot chocolate with whipped cream turns an ordinary evening into an event. The trick is garnish: sugared rims, fruit twists and fresh herbs make a simple juice drink look celebration-ready.

Batch Drinks for Parties

When you are hosting, batch everything. A big pitcher of sangria-style holiday punch or a slow cooker of mulled cider frees you from playing bartender all night. Prepare garnishes in advance: sugared cranberries, citrus wheels and herb sprigs can be done hours ahead and stored in the fridge. Set up a simple self-serve station with glasses, ice and a labeled pitcher so guests help themselves. For a crowd, plan roughly two drinks per person for the first hour and one per hour after, with plenty of non-alcoholic options available.

Presentation and Garnishes

Holiday drinks are half visual. Rim glasses with sugar mixed with a pinch of cinnamon for a frosted look. Skewer cranberries on cocktail picks, freeze them for an hour, and they keep drinks cold while looking like jewels. Fresh rosemary looks like tiny pine branches and adds a subtle aroma. Serve in your nicest glassware, add a cloth napkin, and even a simple drink feels like an occasion. Small details cost almost nothing and photograph beautifully.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make holiday cocktails ahead of time?

Yes. Mulled wine, sangria-style punches and spiced cider all improve after a few hours as the flavors meld. Keep them refrigerated and reheat gently; add sparkling ingredients and fresh garnishes just before serving.

What are good non-alcoholic holiday drinks?

Sparkling apple-cranberry punch, hot spiced cider, candy-cane hot chocolate and ginger-lemon spritzers are all festive and easy. Serve them in the same nice glassware so they feel equally special.

How much should I plan per guest at a party?

A common rule is two drinks per person for the first hour and about one per hour after that. Always stock more non-alcoholic options than you think you need, since many guests alternate.

How do I keep mulled wine warm without overcooking it?

Use the lowest heat setting or a slow cooker on warm. The wine should steam gently but never boil, since boiling cooks off the alcohol and dulls the spices. Warm it through for at least 20 minutes for the flavors to blend.
