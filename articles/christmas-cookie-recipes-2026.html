Christmas Cookie Recipes 2026: 15 Holiday Classics to Bake

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 4 min read

Nothing fills a home with Christmas spirit like a tray of cookies cooling on the counter. Whether you're baking for a cookie exchange, gifting neighbors, or just filling the cookie jar, these 15 holiday classics cover every craving — from crisp cut-outs to chewy spice cookies to no-bake lifesavers for busy weeks.

Classic Cut-Out Sugar Cookies

The quintessential Christmas cookie. Roll the dough evenly (about a quarter-inch thick), chill cut shapes before baking so edges stay sharp, and decorate with royal icing once completely cool. Royal icing made with meringue powder is food-safe and dries hard enough for stacking and gifting. Outline first, then "flood" the inside — and let each layer dry before adding details.

Gingerbread Cookies

Dark, spicy, and sturdy enough for houses: gingerbread dough with molasses, ginger, cinnamon, and cloves rolls out beautifully and holds its shape. Bake a minute less than you think for chewy centers. Decorate simply with white royal icing — dots, squiggles, and smiles beat elaborate piping for classic charm.

Snickerdoodles

Soft, buttery, and rolled in cinnamon sugar — snickerdoodles are the crowd-pleaser that disappears first from every platter. The cream of tartar in the dough gives them their signature tangy flavor and chewy texture. Slightly underbake for centers that stay soft for days.

Shortbread

Three ingredients — butter, sugar, flour — and infinite elegance. Good shortbread is all about quality butter and patience: press the dough firmly into the pan, dock it with a fork, and bake low and slow until just golden at the edges. Dip half in melted chocolate or press with a holiday stamp for a gift-worthy finish.

More Classics Worth Baking

  • Peanut butter blossoms: Soft peanut butter cookies crowned with a chocolate kiss — press the candy in the moment they leave the oven.
  • Russian tea cakes (snowballs): Buttery nut cookies rolled twice in powdered sugar; they look like snowballs on the platter.
  • Peppermint bark: Layers of dark and white chocolate topped with crushed candy canes — technically candy, but it belongs on every cookie tray.
  • Molasses crinkles: Chewy, crackled tops, deep spice flavor — the underrated star of the holiday cookie tin.
  • Thumbprint cookies: Buttery rounds with jam centers; use raspberry or apricot for jewel-like color.
  • Chocolate crinkles: Fudgy centers with crackled powdered-sugar tops — brownie meets cookie.

No-Bake Lifesavers

  • Chocolate-dipped pretzels: Dip in melted chocolate, add sprinkles — sweet, salty, and ready in 20 minutes.
  • Rum balls: Crushed wafers, cocoa, and a splash of rum rolled in powdered sugar; they improve after a day or two of resting.
  • Haystacks: Chow mein noodles or pretzel sticks bound with melted butterscotch — crunchy, nostalgic, effortless.
  • Oreo truffles: Crushed cookies mixed with cream cheese, dipped in chocolate. Three ingredients, endless compliments.

Decorating, Storing, and Gifting Tips

  • Cool completely before decorating: Even slightly warm cookies melt icing into a puddle.
  • Store by texture: Keep crisp cookies and soft cookies in separate containers, or the crisp ones go soft.
  • Freeze for later: Most drop and cut-out cookies freeze beautifully for up to three months — bake early, decorate later.
  • Gift in layers: Line tins or boxes with parchment, separate layers with wax paper, and tuck in a handwritten label with the cookie's name.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance can I bake Christmas cookies?

Most cookies keep 1–2 weeks in an airtight container at room temperature. For longer storage, freeze baked undecorated cookies for up to three months and thaw at room temperature. Royal icing decorations should be added after thawing.

Why do my sugar cookies spread and lose their shape?

The usual culprits are warm dough and too-soft butter. Chill cut shapes for 15–20 minutes before baking, make sure your butter is cool (not melty), and check that your oven is fully preheated so the edges set quickly.

How do I keep cookies soft after baking?

Slightly underbake them — pull cookies when the edges are set but centers look just barely done. Store soft cookies in an airtight container with a slice of bread; the cookies absorb moisture from the bread and stay soft longer.

What are the best cookies for gifting?

Choose sturdy cookies that stack well: shortbread, biscotti, snickerdoodles, gingerbread, and Russian tea cakes all travel beautifully. Avoid heavily frosted or delicate cookies unless you're hand-delivering them.
