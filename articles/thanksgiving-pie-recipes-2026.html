Thanksgiving Pie Recipes 2026: 15 Classic Pies & New Twists

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 7 min read

No Thanksgiving dessert table is complete without pie. Pumpkin, apple, and pecan are the holy trinity, but there is plenty of room for creative twists alongside the classics. The secret to a great pie lineup is planning: most pies can be made ahead, crusts can be frozen for weeks, and a little strategy means you are slicing and serving instead of stressed and baking on the big day. Here are 15 Thanksgiving pie recipes worth a spot on your table.

Pumpkin Pie & Variations

  • Classic pumpkin pie: Canned pumpkin puree, evaporated milk, eggs, sugar, and pumpkin pie spice in a flaky crust. Serve with barely sweetened whipped cream.
  • Bourbon pumpkin pie: A splash of bourbon in the filling deepens the flavor. The alcohol bakes off; the warmth stays.
  • Pumpkin chiffon pie: A lighter, mousse-like filling folded with whipped cream — a nice contrast to the rich dinner.
  • Pumpkin pie bars: The same filling baked in a sheet pan on a shortbread crust, cut into squares. Feeds a crowd with easy serving.

Apple Pie Done Right

  • Classic double-crust apple pie: A mix of tart and sweet apples (like Granny Smith and Honeycrisp), cinnamon, and a squeeze of lemon under a golden top crust.
  • Caramel apple pie: Drizzle homemade or store-bought caramel over the filling before adding the top crust. Serve à la mode.
  • Dutch apple pie: A crumbly streusel topping instead of a top crust — easier to make and a great texture contrast.
  • Mini apple hand pies: Individual pies made with biscuit cutters and pie dough scraps. Perfect for kids and for sampling.

Pecan & Nut Pies

  • Classic pecan pie: Corn syrup, brown sugar, butter, eggs, and pecans in a flaky shell. Rich, gooey, and iconic.
  • Chocolate pecan pie: Add cocoa or chopped chocolate to the filling for a fudgier, deeper pie.
  • Maple walnut pie: A pecan-pie style filling made with maple syrup and walnuts — a New England-inspired twist.
  • Buttermilk pie: A simple custard pie with a tangy, creamy filling and a crackly top. An underrated Southern classic.

Beyond the Big Three

  • Sweet potato pie: Smoother and slightly sweeter than pumpkin, with the same warm spices. A must where it is traditional.
  • Cranberry curd tart: Bright, tart cranberry curd in a buttery tart shell — a beautiful, jewel-toned addition to the table.
  • Chocolate cream pie: Silky chocolate pudding in a baked crust with whipped cream. A no-spice option for the spice-averse.

Make-Ahead, Freezing & Crust Tips

  • Bake ahead: Pumpkin, pecan, and sweet potato pies bake perfectly 1 to 2 days ahead. Cool completely, then refrigerate.
  • Freeze unbaked: Fruit pies can be assembled and frozen unbaked for up to 3 months. Bake straight from frozen, adding 15 to 20 minutes.
  • Crusts freeze beautifully: Make pie dough weeks ahead, flatten into disks, wrap well, and freeze. Thaw overnight in the fridge.
  • Serve at room temperature: Most Thanksgiving pies taste best at room temperature. Pull refrigerated pies out 1 to 2 hours before serving.
  • Keep everything cold: Cold butter, cold water, even a chilled bowl. Cold fat is what makes crusts flaky.
  • Do not overwork the dough: Mix just until it holds together — visible bits of butter are a good thing.
  • Blind bake when needed: Custard and cream pies need a fully baked empty shell. Line with parchment, fill with pie weights or dried beans, and bake until golden.
  • Shield the edges: A pie shield or foil ring keeps crust edges from over-browning during long bakes.
  • Store-bought is fine: A good refrigerated crust saves real time. No one at the table will mind.
  • How many pies: Plan one 9-inch pie per 6 to 8 guests if serving multiple varieties — people always want to taste more than one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make Thanksgiving pies ahead of time?

Yes. Pumpkin, pecan, and sweet potato pies can be baked 1 to 2 days ahead and refrigerated. Fruit pies can be assembled and frozen unbaked up to 3 months ahead, then baked from frozen. Cream pies should be made the day before at the earliest.

How many pies do I need for Thanksgiving?

A good rule is one 9-inch pie per 6 to 8 guests when serving multiple varieties, since people like to try more than one kind. For a crowd of 12 with three pie options, three pies is usually right.

Should Thanksgiving pie be served warm or cold?

Most Thanksgiving pies — pumpkin, pecan, apple — are best at room temperature. Fruit pies can be served slightly warm with ice cream. Pull refrigerated pies out 1 to 2 hours before dessert so the flavors open up.

How do I keep my pie crust from getting soggy?

Blind-bake crusts for custard pies, brush the bottom crust with beaten egg white before filling fruit pies, and bake on a preheated baking sheet on the lowest oven rack so the bottom sets fast. Let pies cool completely on a rack so steam escapes.
