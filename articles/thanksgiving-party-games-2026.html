15 Fun Thanksgiving Party Games 2026: For Kids and Adults

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

After the dishes are cleared on November 26, 2026, the holiday hits a familiar crossroads: half the family drifts toward naps and phones, and the energy of the day quietly evaporates. Games fix that. A good Thanksgiving game pulls three generations to the same table, creates the stories you retell for years, and costs almost nothing. The trick is matching the game to the moment: low-energy games while the turkey rests, active ones after dinner, and quick ones for kids with short attention spans. Here are 15 that actually work.

While the Turkey Rests: Low-Energy Games

The hour before dinner is prime game time because everyone is gathered and nobody wants to move much. Thanksgiving Pictionary needs only paper and a marker: teams draw words like "gravy boat," "wishbone," and "food coma" while a 60-second timer runs. Two Truths and a Thanks puts a holiday spin on the classic: each person shares two true things they are thankful for and one lie, and the table guesses the fib. Gratitude Bingo works for all ages: hand out bingo cards with squares like "someone mentions the dog," "uncle falls asleep," or "a toast is made," and the first to get five in a row wins a prize. These games run themselves and keep the pre-dinner hour from dragging.

After-Dinner Classics for Everyone

Once the plates are cleared, bring out the games with a little more structure. Thanksgiving trivia in teams of 3 to 4, with easy, medium, and hard rounds, reliably gets competitive; see our 50-question trivia guide for ready-made rounds. Turkey feather relay is the physical comedy hit: players race across the room keeping a feather (or a balloon) in the air by blowing on it, no hands allowed. The thankful alphabet game goes around the table with each person naming something they are grateful for starting with the next letter; hesitate more than 5 seconds and you are out. Photo scavenger hunt sends teams around the house finding items like "something older than grandma" or "the weirdest spice in the kitchen" and photographing each. Keep each game to 15 or 20 minutes; ending while people still want more is the secret to a great game night.

Games Just for Kids

Children need games with movement and instant payoffs. Pin the feather on the turkey is the Thanksgiving version of pin the tail on the donkey: draw a big turkey on kraft paper, cut out paper feathers, and blindfold each player. Pumpkin bowling uses mini pumpkins as balls and empty cans wrapped in fall paper as pins down a hallway. Corn kernel scoop race gives each child a spoon and a bowl of dried corn kernels; the one who transfers the most in 60 seconds wins. Thanksgiving freeze dance plays holiday music and freezes everyone mid-dance move when it stops, with the twist that frozen players must hold a "grateful face." Leaf pile treasure hunt hides small wrapped candies in a pile of (clean) leaves outside for a pre-dinner energy burner. Kids remember the games longer than the meal, so it is worth the 10 minutes of setup.

For the Competitive Adults

Once the kids are busy, the adults get their own bracket. Left Right Center with candy corn replaces chips with candy corn and plays in fast 10-minute rounds. Thanksgiving charades with holiday-only prompts ("carving the turkey," "Black Friday stampede," "the Macy's parade") gets louder and funnier every round. The wishbone tournament settles the age-old question properly: save wishbones from the turkey (or buy a few extras), run a single-elimination bracket, and crown a wishbone champion who gets first pick of leftovers. Pie-eating contest with mini pies, hands behind backs, is as messy as it sounds; do it outside or over a tablecloth you do not love. Cap the adult games at two or three so the evening ends on a high note instead of trailing off.

Prizes, Timing, and Hosting Tips

Prizes make every game better, and they do not need to be expensive. Stock a prize basket with:

  • Mini bottles of sparkling cider and fancy chocolate bars.
  • Scratch-off lottery tickets and $5 coffee shop gift cards.
  • Funny trophies from the dollar store, like "Turkey Day Champion" medals.
  • First pick of leftovers, the most coveted prize of all.
  • A "get out of dishes" pass, which adults will fight for.

Time games between courses, not during them: one game while the turkey rests, one after dessert. Announce the game 10 minutes ahead so stragglers gather, and always explain the rules in under 60 seconds; if a game needs a longer explanation, pick a simpler one. Most importantly, let people opt out gracefully. The grumpy uncle who "does not do games" will join the second one voluntarily if nobody pressures him into the first.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best Thanksgiving games for large groups?

Team games scale best: Thanksgiving trivia in teams of 3 to 4, Pictionary with two big teams, and gratitude bingo where everyone plays at once. Avoid games that make 15 people wait their turn.

How do I get shy family members to join in?

Start with low-pressure games like gratitude bingo or trivia where nobody performs alone. Never single anyone out, and let people join the second game voluntarily once they see others having fun.

What games work for toddlers at Thanksgiving?

Simple and physical: pin the feather on the turkey, freeze dance, a leaf treasure hunt, and coloring pages at the kids table. Keep each activity under 10 minutes.

Do Thanksgiving games need supplies?

Most do not. Trivia, charades, the alphabet gratitude game, and two truths need nothing but people. Keep paper, markers, and a timer on hand and you are covered for almost everything.

When should we play games on Thanksgiving Day?

One game while the turkey rests before dinner, and one or two after dessert. Playing between courses keeps energy up without interrupting the meal.
