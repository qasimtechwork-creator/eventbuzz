Christmas Turkey Recipes 2026: Roast Turkey for the Holiday Table

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

Nothing says Christmas quite like a golden roast turkey carried to the table. It is the most traditional holiday centerpiece, and while it has a reputation for being tricky, the formula is simple: buy the right size, brine or butter it generously, roast at the right temperature, and trust a thermometer over the clock. Follow this guide and your 2026 Christmas turkey will be juicy, flavorful, and worthy of applause.

Choosing and Thawing Your Turkey

Plan on 1 to 1.25 pounds of turkey per person, which leaves room for leftovers. A 12 to 14 pound bird serves 10 to 12 guests. Buy fresh if you can find one within a day or two of Christmas; otherwise, buy frozen early and thaw it in the refrigerator, allowing 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds. A 14 pound turkey needs about 3 days to thaw safely.

Never thaw a turkey on the counter. If you are short on time, submerge the wrapped bird in cold water, changing the water every 30 minutes; it thaws in about 30 minutes per pound this way. Remove the giblets and neck from both cavities before you start, and pat the turkey completely dry inside and out.

Brine or Herb Butter: Pick Your Method

Brining is the insurance policy against dry meat. A simple wet brine is 1 cup of kosher salt and 1/2 cup of sugar dissolved per gallon of cold water, plus bay leaves, peppercorns, and orange peel. Submerge the turkey for 12 to 24 hours in the fridge. If brining sounds like too much work, a dry brine of salt and herbs rubbed under and over the skin for 24 hours works nearly as well and needs no bucket.

Herb butter is the flavor layer. Mash 1 cup of softened butter with minced rosemary, thyme, sage, garlic, lemon zest, salt, and pepper. Gently loosen the skin over the breast and thighs with your fingers and push the butter underneath, then rub the rest all over the outside. The butter bastes the meat from within as it roasts.

Roasting Times and Temperatures

Roast at 325°F for the most even results. As a rule of thumb, an unstuffed turkey needs about 13 minutes per pound, and a stuffed one needs about 15 minutes per pound. A 14 pound unstuffed bird takes roughly 3 to 3.5 hours. Tent the breast with foil for the first two-thirds of roasting to keep it from browning too fast, then remove the foil so the skin crisps.

Forget the pop-up timer, which almost always overshoots. A reliable instant-read thermometer is the only tool that guarantees success. Check these targets:

  • Breast meat: 150°F, then rest to carry over to 155°F plus (safe and juicy)
  • Thigh and drumstick: 165°F to 175°F for tender, fully cooked dark meat
  • Stuffing (if cooked inside): 165°F in the center
  • Resting: 30 to 40 minutes tented in foil before carving

Resting is non-negotiable. Cut too early and the juices run out onto the board instead of staying in the meat. Use the resting window to make gravy from the pan drippings and warm the rolls.

Carving Like a Pro

Carving is simpler than it looks. First remove the legs: cut through the skin between the breast and thigh, then pop the joint and slice the leg off. Separate the drumstick from the thigh at its joint. Next, slice the breast meat off the bone in one piece on each side, then cut across the grain into even slices. Arrange white and dark meat on a warmed platter, spoon over a little warm broth to keep it glistening, and garnish with herbs and cranberries.

If you are nervous, carve in the kitchen rather than at the table. Nobody minds, and the slices look better arranged on a platter than hacked apart in front of an audience.

What to Do With Leftovers

Turkey leftovers are a Christmas tradition of their own. The carcass makes an incredible stock for soup; simmer it with vegetables for 4 hours and you have the base for turkey noodle soup. Slice cold turkey for sandwiches with cranberry sauce, fold diced meat into a pot pie, or make a turkey tetrazzini casserole. Refrigerate leftovers within two hours and use within 4 days, or freeze for up to 3 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size turkey do I need for Christmas?

Plan 1 to 1.25 pounds per person. A 12 to 14 pound turkey serves 10 to 12 guests with leftovers.

Should I brine my Christmas turkey?

Brining adds insurance against dry meat, but it is optional. A butter-under-the-skin roast with proper temperature control is also juicy. Dry brining (salt rub) is the easiest middle ground.

How long does it take to roast a turkey?

At 325°F, allow about 13 minutes per pound unstuffed or 15 minutes per pound stuffed. Always verify with a thermometer, not the clock.

What temperature is turkey done?

The thigh should read 165°F. The breast can be pulled at 150°F and will carry over while resting. Stuffing cooked inside must reach 165°F.

Why is my turkey breast dry?

The breast overcooks while the thighs finish. Tent the breast with foil early in roasting, pull the bird based on thigh temperature, and let it rest 30 to 40 minutes.
