Christmas Hairstyles 2026: Party Hair From Bows to Braids

By EventBuzz Team • October 21, 2026 • 10 min read

Hair is the frame every December photograph hangs in, and the good news about christmas hairstyles in 2026 is that the season's favorite looks are built from a handful of learnable techniques: a bow placed well, a braid with its fullness pulled out, waves with the shine left in, a bun that stays where you put it. This guide walks the main style families by occasion and hair type, with honest notes on difficulty, hold and prep, so you can pick a look you can actually execute on the day, not just admire on a screen.

Complete the picture with our Christmas makeup looks and Christmas nail ideas, and if family photographs are the main event, our Christmas photo outfit guide coordinates the whole household. First, the accessory that owns this season.

The Velvet Bow: 2026's Signature Finish

If one accessory defines Christmas hair this year, it is the bow: velvet in red, black or deep green, worn at the back of a half up style, at the end of a braid, over a low ponytail or clipped above a bun. Bows do three jobs at once: they make a simple style look deliberately festive, they photograph beautifully from every angle including the back, which most hairstyles forget, and they require almost no skill to place. A hairstyle plus a bow is the season's highest return on effort.

Sizing and placement are the craft. Large bows suit long, thick hair and simple bases; smaller bows suit fine hair and shorter lengths, and any bow should sit securely on a small ponytail or braid base rather than clipped to loose hair that will slide. Match the bow to your outfit's formality: velvet for evening, satin for dressy daytime, and a simple grosgrain for family casual. If you buy one hair accessory this December, the stylists' verdict is unanimous: make it this one.

Braids: Crowns, Sides and Fishtails

Braids bring structure that survives a whole party, dancing included, and they reward practice more than talent. The crown braid, plaited around the head and pinned, reads romantic and keeps hair completely off the face and neck, ideal for long evenings and warm rooms. The side braid, swept over one shoulder and gently pulled apart for fullness, is the fastest festive transformation for long hair. The fishtail braid looks intricate and is honestly just two strands alternated, slower than a classic braid but not harder.

The professional trick with every braid is pancaking: after securing, gently tug each loop outward to widen and soften the braid, which doubles its apparent volume and turns neat into styled. Braids work across textures, holding particularly well in hair with some grip, which is why day two hair braids better than freshly washed silkiness. A bow or a few pearl pins at the braid's end finishes the Christmas story.

Waves and Curls: The Party Default

Loose waves are the most requested party hairstyle in the world because they suit nearly every face, length below the chin, and outfit. The 2026 version is soft and shiny rather than tight and set: large sections, a wide barrel or rollers, curls brushed out into connected waves with the ends left a touch straighter for modernity. Hollywood style waves, all curling in one direction with a deep side part, are the glamorous end of the same family and the right choice with a red lip from our makeup guide.

Technique notes that matter: curl away from the face at the front, alternate directions through the back for a natural finish, let every curl cool completely before brushing, because warm curls brushed early are waves that quit by dinner, and always use heat protection, since December often means styling several days in a row. Curly and coily hair can join this family by defining and stretching natural curls rather than fighting them, with shine and shape as the goals.

Updos and Buns: Sleek, Twisted and Off the Neck

Updos are the formal answer and the practical one for long dinners, dancing and outfits with interesting necklines or backs that deserve an audience. The sleek low bun, center or side parted, smoothed and polished, is the season's most elegant option and pairs with statement earrings like nothing else. The twisted updo and French twist soften the same idea for a romantic rather than architectural effect. The high bun and topknot bring energy and suit younger, dressier parties.

Honest difficulty notes: sleek styles show every flyaway and demand product and patience; twisted styles forgive more and can be learned from one practice run. All updos share the same insurance policy: more pins than feels necessary, placed in crossed pairs, a light mist of strong hold spray, and a test shake of the head over the sink before you leave. An updo that survives the test survives the party.

Ponytails and Half Up Styles: The Middle Path

The dressed up ponytail is December's hardest working style. A low ponytail with a wrapped section of hair hiding the band, finished with a velvet bow, reads polished in minutes. A high, snatched ponytail with the lengths waved brings red carpet energy to a party dress. Bubble ponytails, banded at intervals and puffed between bands, are the playful option younger wearers love and they photograph wonderfully.

Half up styles split the difference between wearing hair down and controlling it: the front sections pulled back into a small braid, twist, clip or bow, the lengths left in waves. They suit medium and long hair, every texture, and faces that find full updos severe. For family photographs, half up styles are the stylists' quiet favorite, because hair still frames the face in the picture while staying out of the food, the candles and the children's hands in real life.

Short Hair and Fine Hair: Your Best Moves

Short hair is not a limitation at Christmas; it is a style with the editing already done. Bobs and lobs take waves beautifully with a small barrel, pixies shine with a deep side part and a glossy finish, and every short cut is transformed by accessories: a velvet bow on a small clip, pearl or jeweled pins stacked above one ear, or a slim headband in a festive fabric. One strong accessory on short hair reads as a complete hairstyle, because there is less hair competing for attention.

Fine hair's strategy is texture and trickery: volumizing prep at the roots, styles that build in grip like braids and twists, teasing kept gentle and hidden at the crown, and accessories scaled down so they do not overwhelm or slide. Dry texturizing products are fine hair's December friend, and so is the honest acceptance that a smaller, neater version of a style often looks more expensive than a big one collapsing by nine o'clock.

Curly, Coily and Protective Styles

Natural texture is a party asset, and the season's looks work with it rather than against it. Defined curls worn full and glossy, a pineapple updo that shows off volume, twist outs with a bow or jeweled clip, and braided protective styles dressed with cuffs, ribbon threaded through, or a bow at the crown all belong at the celebration exactly as they are. Moisture and definition are the prep priorities, with satin at night to keep the style across several events.

Wigs, weaves and extensions follow the same style families in this guide, waves, buns, ponytails and bows, with attention to secure installation for a long evening. Whatever the hair's origin, the December rules are universal: rehearse the style once, secure it more than feels necessary, and choose the version you can forget about once it is done, because the best party hair is the hair you stop thinking about.

Pack the smallest emergency kit in your bag: two pins, a mini band, and a travel spray or smoothing wipe for flyaways. And rehearse: every style in this guide is a first attempt for someone this December, and the difference between the tutorial and the triumph is almost always one practice run on a quiet evening, with photographs, in party light, exactly like your makeup. Give your hair that rehearsal and Christmas Day styling becomes the calmest part of getting ready.

Conclusion

Bows for instant festivity, braids for structure, waves for romance, buns for elegance, and a rehearsal for all of them: Christmas hairstyles in 2026 are less about chasing a perfect picture and more about choosing the look your hair, your outfit and your evening agree on. Pick your family, practice it once, pin it generously, and then go and enjoy the party you got ready for.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular Christmas hairstyle for 2026?

The velvet bow leads the season, worn with half up styles, low ponytails and braids, because it makes simple hair look festive with almost no skill. Loose brushed out waves remain the most requested base style, and crown braids and sleek low buns lead the structured looks.

What is an easy Christmas hairstyle for long hair?

A side braid, gently pulled apart for fullness and finished with a bow, is the fastest festive style for long hair. A low ponytail with the band wrapped in hair and a velvet bow added runs a close second, and both survive a whole evening without attention.

How do I make party curls last all night?

Use heat protection, curl in sections, and let every curl cool completely before touching it, because brushing warm curls is what makes waves collapse. Brush out only when cold, finish with a light hold spray, and avoid overloading hair with heavy products beforehand.

What Christmas hairstyles suit short hair?

Waves with a small barrel, a deep side part with a glossy finish, and one strong accessory, a velvet bow clip, stacked pearl pins or a festive headband, all work beautifully on short hair. A single bold accessory reads as a complete hairstyle when there is less hair competing for attention.

Should I wash my hair on the day of a Christmas party?

Usually not for styled looks. Day two hair holds braids, updos and curls better because it has natural grip. If you wash on the day, add dry shampoo or texture spray to recreate that grip, and give any style a full rehearsal in advance so the day itself holds no surprises.
