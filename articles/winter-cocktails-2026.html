12 Cozy Winter Cocktails 2026: Warm Drinks for Cold Nights

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

Winter drinking is about warmth in every sense: hot mugs cradled in cold hands, spices that smell like the holidays, and drinks that slow the evening down. These 12 winter cocktails range from fireside classics to party-ready batches, each with a mocktail path so nobody is left out. Stock the spices once, and you are set for the season.

Hot Classics (1-4)

1. Classic mulled wine: Simmer (never boil) a bottle of fruity red wine with sliced orange, 6 cloves, 2 cinnamon sticks, 2 star anise, and a quarter cup of honey for 20 minutes. Strain and serve warm. 2. Hot toddy: The cold-weather cure-all: 2 oz bourbon or whiskey, a tablespoon of honey, half an ounce of lemon juice, topped with hot water, garnished with a lemon wheel studded with cloves. 3. Irish coffee: Preheat a mug, add 1.5 oz Irish whiskey and a teaspoon of brown sugar, fill with hot strong coffee, and float lightly whipped cream on top. Drink through the cream. 4. Hot buttered rum: Mash together butter, brown sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg, and a pinch of salt into a batter; stir a tablespoon into hot water with 2 oz dark rum.

Spiced and Seasonal (5-8)

5. Spiked apple cider: Warm fresh cider with cinnamon sticks, cloves, and orange peel, then add bourbon or spiced rum to taste, about 1.5 oz per mug. 6. Cranberry bourbon smash: Muddle fresh cranberries with rosemary and honey, add 2 oz bourbon and half an ounce of orange liqueur, shake with ice, and top with ginger beer. Festive red and endlessly sippable. 7. Eggnog martini: Shake 2 oz vanilla vodka, 2 oz good eggnog, and half an ounce of amaretto with ice; strain into a chilled glass and dust with nutmeg. 8. Winter sangria: Combine red wine with brandy, sliced persimmon, pomegranate seeds, orange, and cinnamon sticks; chill 4 hours so the fruit infuses.

Rich Dessert Drinks (9-12)

9. Spiked hot chocolate: Melt real dark chocolate into warm milk with a pinch of salt, then add peppermint schnapps, Baileys, or bourbon. Top with whipped cream. 10. Coquito: Puerto Rico's coconut eggnog: blend coconut milk, cream of coconut, evaporated milk, rum, cinnamon, and vanilla. Serve chilled; it keeps two weeks refrigerated and improves with age. 11. Espresso martini, winter edition: The classic vodka-espresso-coffee liqueur build, with a quarter ounce of cinnamon syrup added for the season. 12. Smoked maple old fashioned: Stir 2 oz bourbon with a bar spoon of maple syrup and two dashes of bitters over a big cube; garnish with an orange twist and, if you are feeling theatrical, a wisp of applewood smoke under a cloche.

Batching for Parties

Winter parties are made for batched drinks, and mulled wine, spiked cider, coquito, and sangria all scale beautifully. The rule of thumb: multiply everything except ice, and hold fresh citrus and carbonated toppers until serving so they stay bright. Keep hot batches warm in a slow cooker on low, with a ladle and a stack of mugs beside it, a self-serve station that frees you to enjoy your own party. Label the ABV clearly if you are also serving mocktails.

  • Slow cooker method: Combine mulled wine or cider ingredients, cook on low 2 to 3 hours, then hold on warm.
  • Make-ahead: Coquito and sangria improve after a day in the fridge; mix them the night before.
  • Garnish station: Cinnamon sticks, orange twists, cranberries, rosemary sprigs, and star anise let guests customize.
  • Glassware: Heatproof mugs for hot drinks; pre-chill glasses for the cold ones.

Easy Mocktail Versions

Every drink above has a zero-proof twin that is genuinely good, not an afterthought. Mulled wine becomes mulled grape or pomegranate juice with the same spices. Hot toddies work with honey, lemon, and hot water plus a strong brewed tea. Spiked cider is excellent plain. Cranberry-rosemary spritzers with ginger beer cover the festive-red slot, and a "nog" of oat milk, vanilla, cinnamon, and nutmeg satisfies the eggnog craving. Serve mocktails in the same nice glassware with the same garnishes; inclusion is in the details. Whatever you pour, winter's real ingredient is unhurried company, so make a round, settle in, and let the cold stay outside where it belongs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the easiest winter cocktail for a party?

Mulled wine or spiked apple cider in a slow cooker. Combine everything, set to low, and let guests serve themselves. Both scale easily and fill the house with amazing aroma.

Can I make mulled wine ahead of time?

Yes, make it a day ahead and refrigerate, then reheat gently without boiling. Never boil mulled wine or the alcohol cooks off and the spices turn bitter.

What are good non-alcoholic winter drinks?

Mulled grape or pomegranate juice, hot honey-lemon-ginger tea, plain spiked-style cider, cranberry rosemary spritzers, and oat milk nog with cinnamon all work beautifully.

How do I keep hot cocktails warm at a party?

A slow cooker on the warm setting is ideal for mulled wine and cider. Preheat mugs with hot water so drinks stay warm longer after serving.

What spices do I need for winter cocktails?

Cinnamon sticks, whole cloves, star anise, and fresh nutmeg cover nearly every recipe. Buy them whole; they keep for a year and taste far better than ground.
