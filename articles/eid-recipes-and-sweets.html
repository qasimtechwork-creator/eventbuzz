Eid Recipes & Sweets: Traditional Dishes to Celebrate Eid

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Ask anyone what Eid tastes like and the answer arrives instantly: sheer khurma in the morning, biryani at the big table, and something sweet within reach all day long. After a month of fasting, the Eid menu is a celebration in itself, and most of it can be planned and partly prepared before the moon is even sighted. Below are the traditional dishes that anchor an Eid table, each with a simple ingredient and method outline in plain words so you can cook with confidence. Quick answer: A classic Eid menu runs in three acts: sheer khurma or seviyan after the Eid prayer, biryani and kebabs for the main feast, and easy sweets like dates, cookies and kheer for visitors all day. Prepare the dessert base and marinate the kebabs on Chand Raat to keep Eid morning calm.

Sheer Khurma: The Sweet Start to Eid

Sheer khurma is the dish Eid morning is famous for: fine vermicelli simmered in sweetened milk with dates and nuts. To make a family pot, you will need full-cream milk, a handful of very fine vermicelli noodles, sugar, soft dates, and a mix of sliced almonds, pistachios and cashews, with a little ghee and a pinch of cardamom. Warm the ghee and lightly fry the vermicelli until golden and fragrant, then set it aside. Bring the milk to a gentle boil, add sugar and the chopped dates, and let it simmer until slightly thickened. Stir in the vermicelli and most of the nuts and cook a few more minutes until soft. Serve warm or chilled with the remaining nuts on top. It tastes even better the next day, so a Chand Raat batch is a wise move.

Seviyan: The Quick Dry Vermicelli Dessert

Where sheer khurma is milky, seviyan is its drier, quicker cousin, roasted vermicelli tossed with sugar and nuts, ready in minutes when guests arrive unannounced. Break the vermicelli into short lengths and roast it in ghee, stirring constantly, until it turns a deep golden colour. Splash in a little water or milk, just enough for the strands to soften, then fold through sugar, cardamom and roasted nuts and let it rest off the heat so the sweetness settles. Serve in small bowls with tea. Because it uses store-cupboard ingredients and no chilling time, seviyan is the Eid cook's best friend during a busy visiting day.

Biryani: The Centrepiece of the Feast

No Eid table feels complete without a handi of biryani opened at the table, releasing its steam of saffron and spice. The outline is simple, even if the dish feels grand. Marinate chicken or beef in yogurt, fried onions, ginger, garlic and your favourite biryani spices. Separately, parboil long-grain basmati rice in salted water with whole spices until it is almost done, then drain it. Layer rice and meat in a heavy pot, finishing with rice on top, dotted with ghee, saffron-tinted milk or a little food colouring, fried onions, mint and coriander. Seal the lid and cook on the lowest heat, the gentle steam finish called dum, until the rice is tender and the grains separate. Rest it before serving, and bring out raita and salad alongside.

Kebabs: For Snacking and Sharing

Kebabs cover everything between meals: the plate passed around when cousins arrive, the extra dish that makes the feast feel abundant. For simple seekh-style kebabs, mix minced beef or chicken with finely chopped onion, green chillies, fresh coriander, ginger, garlic and ground spices such as cumin, coriander and garam masala, plus salt. Rest the mixture so it firms up, shape it around skewers or into small patties, and grill, bake or shallow-fry until cooked through and lightly charred at the edges. Serve with mint chutney, onion rings and lemon wedges. Shami kebabs, made from minced meat cooked with split chickpeas and spices then ground, shaped and fried, keep beautifully and can be prepared days ahead and frozen.

Easy Sweets to Keep All Day

Beyond the two vermicelli classics, a few low-effort sweets keep the Eid table generous from morning to midnight. A simple rice kheer, slow-cooked milk and rice sweetened and scented with cardamom, chills well and serves a crowd. Stuffed dates, filled with chopped nuts or a little nut paste, take minutes and look elegant on a tray. Plain butter cookies or coconut sweets from your own family tradition round out the spread, and a bowl of fresh fruit helps everyone pace themselves. The rule is generosity without exhaustion: two homemade stars, a couple of easy extras, and the kettle always ready. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most traditional sweet eaten on Eid?

Sheer khurma, vermicelli cooked in sweetened milk with dates and nuts, is the classic Eid al-Fitr morning dish in South Asian homes and the first sweet many families eat after the Eid prayer.

What is the difference between sheer khurma and seviyan?

Both use vermicelli. Sheer khurma is simmered in milk with dates for a rich, pourable dessert, while seviyan is roasted and lightly moistened for a drier, quicker sweet served in small portions.

What food is served first on Eid morning?

Traditionally something sweet, often dates and sheer khurma, after returning from the Eid prayer, followed later by the main feast of biryani, kebabs and more desserts through the day.

Can Eid dishes be prepared in advance?

Yes. Sheer khurma and kheer improve overnight in the fridge, kebab mixtures and shami kebabs can be made and frozen ahead, and biryani can be layered in advance and given its final steam before serving.

What should I cook for guests who arrive all day?

Keep seviyan, stuffed dates, cookies and tea ready for drop-in visitors, and save the biryani for the main family meal. Small, sweet and ready beats elaborate and late.

Conclusion

Eid food is memory food: the same dishes, made with love, year after year. Start the day with sheer khurma, crown the table with biryani and kebabs, and keep easy sweets flowing for everyone who knocks.
