Thanksgiving Movie Night 2026: 25+ Best Family Movies to Watch After the Feast

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

After the plates are cleared and the pie is served, the best part of Thanksgiving begins: everyone piling onto couches and armchairs for a movie. The post-feast movie is a sacred tradition in many families, equal parts entertainment and digestion strategy. Here are 25+ of the best movies for Thanksgiving movie night 2026, from actual Thanksgiving films to cozy classics the whole family will love.

Classic Thanksgiving Movies

A few films are genuinely about Thanksgiving. "Planes, Trains and Automobiles" (1987) is the gold standard: Steve Martin and John Candy's disastrous journey home for the holiday is the funniest Thanksgiving movie ever made and still the most quoted. "A Charlie Brown Thanksgiving" (1973) is the family-friendly essential, only 25 minutes long, perfect for the youngest viewers before the main feature. "Miracle on 34th Street" (1947) technically opens on Thanksgiving Day with the Macy's parade, making it the perfect bridge from Thanksgiving into the Christmas season. "You've Got Mail" (1998) features one of the great cozy Thanksgiving dinner scenes in film history.

Family Comedies Everyone Can Agree On

The post-dinner crowd spans ages 8 to 80, so pick comedies with broad appeal. "Home Alone" (1990) works in late November because it is really an autumn holiday movie at heart. "The Princess Bride" (1987) is the rare film that grandparents, parents, and kids all love equally. "Night at the Museum" (2006), "Mrs. Doubtfire" (1993), and "The Addams Family" (1991) all deliver laughs without anything that will make the room uncomfortable. For a newer pick, "Wonka" (2023) is pure cozy sweetness that fits the holiday mood perfectly.

Animated Favorites for Mixed Ages

Animation is the secret weapon of family movie night because it entertains every generation. "Coco" (2017) is about family, memory, and gratitude, making it arguably the most thematically perfect Thanksgiving movie ever made. "Ratatouille" (2007) celebrates food and cooking, which feels exactly right after a feast. "Toy Story" (1995), "Inside Out" (2015), and "Up" (2009) are all-timers that hold up for adults. If you want something autumnal, "Fantastic Mr. Fox" (2009) has that warm harvest color palette and dry wit that plays great for grown-ups.

Cozy and Heartwarming Picks

  • Forrest Gump (1994): A sweeping, feel-good story about kindness and perseverance.
  • The Blind Side (2009): Family, football, and Thanksgiving itself feature prominently.
  • Chef (2014): A joyful food movie that will make everyone hungry again.
  • Julie & Julia (2009): Celebrates cooking with pure delight.
  • Little Women (2019): Warm, literary, and perfect for a chilly evening.
  • The Greatest Showman (2017): Big songs, big heart, impossible not to enjoy.

Tips for the Perfect Movie Night

Set the room before dinner ends: dim the lamps, drag in extra blankets and floor cushions, and have the movie queued up so there is no 20-minute scrolling debate while everyone gets sleepy. Let the kids vote on the shortlist earlier in the day to avoid arguments. Keep the pie and coffee coming during the film, and lower expectations about finishing: it is completely normal, even traditional, for at least one family member to fall asleep. That is not a failed movie night. That is a successful Thanksgiving.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular Thanksgiving movie?

"Planes, Trains and Automobiles" (1987) is widely considered the definitive Thanksgiving comedy, while "A Charlie Brown Thanksgiving" is the most beloved family special. Both are perennial favorites.

What is a good Thanksgiving movie for kids?

"A Charlie Brown Thanksgiving" (25 minutes), "Coco," "Ratatouille," and "Toy Story" are all excellent. They are short enough or engaging enough for young attention spans and enjoyable for adults too.

Are there any Thanksgiving horror or thriller movies?

"Thanksgiving" (2023) is a slasher film set on the holiday, but it is very much adults-only and not a family pick. For the family crowd, stick with the comedies and animated films above.

What movie bridges Thanksgiving and Christmas?

"Miracle on 34th Street" opens on Thanksgiving Day at the Macy's parade and ends at Christmas, making it the perfect transition movie for the holiday season.

How do I pick a movie the whole family agrees on?

Shortlist 4 to 5 options across genres earlier in the day and let everyone vote. Animation and broad comedies are the safest bets for mixed ages, and having the movie queued before dinner prevents the scrolling debate.
