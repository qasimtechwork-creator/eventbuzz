Black Friday Shopping Tips 2026: 15 Tricks to Score the Best Deals

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

Black Friday on November 27, 2026 rewards the prepared and punishes the impulsive. The difference between a great deal and a marketing trick often comes down to a few simple habits: tracking prices before November, knowing store policies, and having a plan before the ads drop. These 15 tricks cover the full playbook, from early preparation to checkout-day tactics to what to do after you buy.

Before November: Preparation Tricks

The best Black Friday shoppers start in October. First, make a list of what you actually want to buy, with specific models, not just categories. "55-inch QLED TV" beats "a TV" because it lets you track the exact price. Second, start a price history now: check the current price of each item and screenshot it, so you can tell a real discount from an inflated "was" price on Black Friday.

Third, sign up for retailer newsletters and loyalty programs in October. Many stores send early-access codes and subscriber-only deals during Black Friday week, and loyalty members often get to shop doorbusters hours before the general public. Fourth, download the apps of your top five retailers and enable push notifications for deal alerts; the fastest sellers go to whoever sees the notification first.

The 15 Tricks

  • 1. Track prices for 30 days first. Use a price tracker or manual screenshots so you know the true discount. A real deal is 20 percent or more off the October price.
  • 2. Set a budget per person or category. Decide your total spend before the ads tempt you. Shoppers with a written budget spend significantly less on impulse buys.
  • 3. Join free loyalty programs. Target Circle, My Best Buy, and store rewards unlock member pricing, points, and early access at zero cost.
  • 4. Shop early November sales. Many retailers run Black Friday prices for the whole month. Buying early gets you better stock and delivery slots.
  • 5. Compare at least three retailers. No single store wins every category. Check prices across major retailers before committing.
  • 6. Read the doorbuster fine print. Limited-quantity deals may be in-store only, one per customer, or excluded from price matching. Know the rules before you queue.
  • 7. Use store pickup to lock inventory. Ordering online for in-store pickup guarantees the item at the sale price without shipping delays.
  • 8. Stack coupons and cashback. Manufacturer coupons, store coupons, credit card rewards, and cashback portals can combine for 5 to 15 percent extra off.
  • 9. Buy discounted gift cards first. Retailers often sell gift cards at 10 to 20 percent off during Black Friday week; use them to pay for your deals.
  • 10. Check the return window before buying. Holiday returns usually extend into January, but doorbusters and clearance items may be final sale.
  • 11. Do not chase every deal. A discount on something you were not going to buy is not savings. Stick to your list.
  • 12. Watch for price adjustments. If an item you bought drops further during the return window, most major retailers will refund the difference.
  • 13. Shop Cyber Monday for leftovers. Many Black Friday prices return on Monday, November 30, with online-exclusive extras.
  • 14. Protect big purchases. Use a credit card with purchase protection and extended warranty benefits on electronics and appliances.
  • 15. Keep all receipts digitally. Store receipts in retailer apps or email so returns and price adjustments are painless.

On the Day: Checkout Tactics

On Black Friday itself, speed and focus win. Shop online first for doorbusters, because the best ones sell out within minutes of going live; have your payment info and shipping address saved in advance so checkout takes seconds. If you are going in store, arrive before opening for limited-quantity items, but skip the lines for anything available online at the same price.

Keep your list open on your phone and check off items as you buy them. It is easy to get distracted by endcaps and flash sales, so refer back to the list between purchases. If a deal looks too good, do a 30-second sanity check: search the model number to confirm it is the current version and not a stripped-down derivative made just for Black Friday.

After You Buy: Protecting Your Deals

The work is not over at checkout. First, re-check prices during the return window; if anything you bought drops further, claim the price adjustment. Second, test electronics and appliances immediately rather than leaving them boxed until Christmas, so any defects surface while returns are easy. Third, register warranties on big-ticket items within the first week.

Finally, review your total spending against the budget you set. If you stayed on list and on budget, you won Black Friday. If you overspent, return the impulse buys now while the extended holiday window is open. The extended January return deadline is your safety net; use it.

Spotting Fake Deals

Not every Black Friday price is a deal. Watch for these red flags: a "was" price far above what the item sold for in October, a model number you cannot find reviewed anywhere (often a Black Friday-only derivative with cheaper components), and bundle "savings" that include accessories you would never buy separately. When in doubt, search the exact model number plus the word "review" to see what the product really is and what it normally costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the single best Black Friday shopping trick?

Track prices for 30 days before November. Knowing the real October price is the only way to tell a genuine discount from an inflated markdown.

Should I shop Black Friday online or in store?

Online for speed and selection, in store for open-box deals and immediate pickup. Prices are usually identical, so choose based on what you are buying.

Do Black Friday deals really sell out?

Doorbusters do, often within hours. Regular sale prices on most items last through the weekend and frequently return on Cyber Monday.

Can I get a refund if the price drops after I buy?

Most major retailers offer price adjustments within the return window. Keep your receipt and ask at customer service or check the retailer's app.

Is Cyber Monday better than Black Friday?

Neither is universally better. Black Friday wins on doorbusters and in-store deals; Cyber Monday wins on online-exclusive codes and travel deals. Shop both.
