Minute to Win It Christmas Games 2026: 60-Second Challenges for Any Party

By EventBuzz Team • October 21, 2026 • 8 min read

Minute to win it games are the espresso shot of a Christmas party: sixty seconds, one silly skill, a room suddenly on its feet shouting encouragement. The format works because the rules fit in one sentence, failure is funny and instant, and nobody needs talent — balancing, stacking, scooping and shaking levels every player in the room. This guide collects the best minute to win it christmas games for 2026, each with supplies, setup and exactly how to run the minute, plus a scoring system that turns separate challenges into one championship.

Use these challenges as the energy layer inside a bigger games night from our party games guide, between rounds of trivia, or as the main event for families using our family games guide. Host rules for every challenge below: demonstrate once, start the timer on GO, and let the crowd count down the last ten seconds out loud — that countdown is half the entertainment.

How the Format Works: Teams, Timers and Points

Run challenges head-to-head or one player at a time against the clock. For parties, teams of four to six rotating a different player per challenge keep everyone involved and rest voices between turns. Scoring that works: 3 points for completing the challenge inside the minute, 1 point for the best attempt among those who do not finish, and a bonus point whenever a player beats the host demonstration. Display the running score where everyone can see it.

Supplies across this whole guide are deliberately household-grade: candy canes, marshmallows, cotton balls, plastic cups, ornaments, straws, paper plates, a stocking and a timer. Set up each station before the party or during a drinks break, rehearse nothing, and film the finals — sixty-second videos of friends failing gloriously at cookie-face are the social content of the season.

Candy Cane Hook-Em: The Opener

Supplies: a bowl of candy canes, one cane per player to hold in the mouth, an empty bowl. Holding a candy cane in their mouth as a hook, the player transfers canes one at a time from the full bowl to the empty bowl — no hands. Most canes moved in 60 seconds wins. The hooked shape does all the design work: simple to understand in five seconds, genuinely tricky by cane four, and hilarious to watch.

Run it as the first challenge of the night because spectators instantly understand it and start coaching. Keep spare canes — snapped hooks are part of the comedy, and a fresh cane mid-round is a fair house rule if stated before the timer starts.

Ornament Stack and Cup Tower Challenges

Ornament stack — supplies: 6 round ornaments per player and a flat tray. Stack six ornaments into a pyramid (three, two, one) without any support; the stack must stand three seconds after the timer or on completion. Shatterproof ornaments only, and a tray boundary keeps escapees contained. Cup tower — supplies: 6 plastic cups. Build a pyramid, then — the twist — take it back down into a single stacked column before the minute ends. Up is easy; the controlled collapse is where rounds are won and lost.

Both challenges reward calm hands under ridiculous pressure, which makes them perfect spectator sport. Play them seated at a table for mixed-mobility groups, and let two players race side by side with identical setups for double the noise.

Marshmallow Scoop and Cotton Ball Relay

Marshmallow scoop — supplies: a bowl of mini marshmallows, a spoon held in the mouth, an empty cup. No hands: scoop and transfer as many marshmallows as possible in the minute. Cotton ball relay — supplies: cotton balls, two bowls, a spoon, optional blindfold for adults. Spoon cotton balls from one bowl to another; they weigh almost nothing, which is the entire joke — players cannot feel whether the spoon is loaded, and the room loses its mind. Count only balls resting in the target bowl when time expires.

These two are the most family-friendly challenges in the guide: no breakables, no mess that matters, and a five-year-old has a genuine chance against an adult in the cotton ball round. They pair beautifully with the gentler games in our family games guide for a mixed-age championship.

Cookie Face and Stocking Guessing Games

Cookie face — supplies: one round cookie per player. Cookie starts on the forehead; using only face muscles, the player walks it down into their mouth. Catch it and eat it inside the minute for full points. It never stops being funny, it photographs brilliantly, and it gives the crowd a single clear hero journey per player. Stocking guess — supplies: a stocking filled with 8 to 10 familiar household or Christmas objects, tied closed. By touch alone through the fabric, the player names as many items as possible in 60 seconds; one point per correct item.

Cookie face suits the middle of the night when dignity has relaxed; stocking guess suits the start or the seated crowd, and doubles as a calm challenge between loud ones. Rotate the stocking contents between rounds if the same group plays twice, or the second team gains an unfair memory advantage.

Snowball Shake and Jingle Bell Toss

Snowball shake — supplies: an empty tissue box or similar container strapped at the waist, filled with 8 cotton balls or lightweight foam balls. On GO, the player shakes, jumps and twists to empty the box — no hands touching it. First empty box, or most balls out in the minute, wins. It is the loudest sixty seconds in this guide and the one to save for peak energy. Jingle bell toss — supplies: a muffin tin or egg carton as the target, small bells or soft balls. Bounce or toss bells into the target zones from a marked line; different zones score differently, highest total in the minute wins.

Give the shake challenge floor space and dignity waivers — nobody has ever looked composed doing it, which is precisely its value as the party legendary moment. The bell toss is its calmer sibling, ideal for seated players, grandparents and anyone who prefers aim over acrobatics.

Ribbon Wrap Relay and Straw Snowball Race

Ribbon wrap — supplies: a roll of ribbon or crepe streamer per pair. One partner wraps the other from shoulders to knees like a Christmas mummy, neatly, against the clock; the neatest full wrap judged at the buzzer takes bonus points. Swap roles for round two. Straw snowball race — supplies: a straw per player, one cotton ball, a table. Players blow their cotton snowball down the table and back; hands may only steady the straw. First home, or furthest along the course at sixty seconds, wins.

Both challenges shine in team relays, where the next teammate starts the moment the first finishes their leg. Relays multiply participation without multiplying supplies, and they create the passing-the-baton drama that makes a room chant names — the true sound of a party working.

Championship Scoring, Prizes and Hosting Honesty

Crown an overall champion with a simple running total and a deliberately ridiculous trophy — a golden spoon, a ribbon medal, a Santa hat the winner wears for the group photo. Prizes stay small: the trophy, first dessert pick, or choosing the next game. Post scores publicly, celebrate last place with the same volume as first (the Best Effort award exists for a reason), and film only with a cheerful announcement so nobody performs unknowingly.

Pros: unmatched energy per minute, near-zero cost, every age and fitness level has a challenge that suits them. Cons: noise, mild chaos and a floor that will need sweeping — schedule challenges before the quiet end of the evening, keep water nearby, and retire any challenge that a particular group finds frustrating rather than funny. For the adults-only after-party, continue with our games for adults.

Conclusion

Sixty seconds is all a great challenge needs: one clear goal, household supplies and a crowd counting down. Rotate players through hook-em canes, stacking, scooping, cookie face and the legendary snowball shake, keep a running score, and your party gains a championship storyline guests will talk about until next Christmas.

Frequently Asked Questions

What supplies do I need for minute to win it Christmas games?

Household basics cover the whole set: candy canes, mini marshmallows, cotton balls, plastic cups, shatterproof ornaments, straws, paper plates, spoons, a stocking, an empty tissue box and a phone timer. Nothing needs buying beyond treats and cotton balls for most homes.

How many players can take part?

Any number, organized in teams of four to six rotating a different player per challenge. Head-to-head pairs speed things up for small groups; for large parties, run two identical stations side by side.

How should I score a minute to win it championship?

Award 3 points for completing a challenge inside the minute, 1 point for the best incomplete attempt, and display a running total. Finish with a trophy moment — a golden spoon or medal — and a Best Effort award so every placing gets celebrated.

Are these games suitable for kids and grandparents?

Most are. Choose the scoop, toss, straw race and stocking guess challenges for mixed ages, use shatterproof ornaments, allow seated play, and adjust distances. Save the snowball shake for willing, mobile players.

How long does a full minute to win it session take?

Eight challenges with setup and scoring take about 45 to 60 minutes. For a shorter burst, pick four challenges — hook-em, cookie face, cotton balls and the shake finale — for a tight 25-minute championship.
