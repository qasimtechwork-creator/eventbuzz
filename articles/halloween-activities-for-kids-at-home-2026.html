Halloween Activities for Kids at Home 2026: Crafts, Games & Fun Ideas

Published October 9, 2026 · Updated October 9, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Not every Halloween needs a street full of trick-or-treaters. Some of the best October memories happen at the kitchen table: painting pumpkins, stringing paper bats across the living room, and eating orange-frosted biscuits while a friendly ghost movie plays. For Halloween 2026, this guide collects the best at-home activities for kids — crafts, games, kitchen fun, and toddler-friendly play — most of them screen-free and built from things you already own.

Everything here works in apartments and houses, in any weather, and in places where door-to-door trick-or-treating is not part of local tradition. Many EventBuzz families in Pakistan, India, and the Gulf celebrate exactly this way: costumes at home, video calls with cousins, and a house that looks delightfully haunted by dinner time. Choose a handful of activities and build your own celebration.

Craft Activities (Set Up Once, Play for Days)

No-Carve Pumpkin Decorating

Skip the knives and the mess. Paint pumpkins with skin-safe acrylics or poster paints, glue on googly eyes and felt features, wrap one in bandage-style cloth strips for a mummy pumpkin, or cover it in pressed autumn leaves and glue. No-carve pumpkins last for weeks and suit every age from toddler up.

Paper Bat & Ghost Garland

Fold black paper, cut one bat shape, and unfold a whole colony. White paper ghosts need only drawn faces. String them on thread, tape them "flying" up a wall or across a doorway, and let children arrange the flock themselves.

Lantern Jars

Coat clean glass jars with orange or white glue-and-tissue layers, add paper faces, and place a battery tea light inside (never a real candle in a child's craft). Line them along a windowsill for an evening glow parade.

Handprint Keepsakes

White handprints become ghosts, orange ones become pumpkins, and black ones become spiders with drawn legs. Add the year, and grandparents receive the sweetest Halloween mail imaginable.

Spooky Slime (Supervised Kitchen Science)

Basic glue-and-activator slime tinted orange or green fascinates ages six and up. Set clear rules — slime lives on the tray, not on the sofa — and store it in sealed tubs. Always supervise, and keep slime away from toddlers who mouth objects.

Kitchen Activities Kids Can Actually Do

  • Mummy biscuits: children drizzle white icing "bandages" over plain biscuits and add two sweet eyes.
  • Pumpkin patch cups: crushed biscuit "soil," chocolate or cocoa pudding, and one pumpkin sweet on top.
  • Monster fruit cups: kiwi, banana, and grape faces arranged in clear cups — the healthy guest at the party.
  • Witches' brew smoothies: spinach-banana-orange blends poured into decorated cups; the green is the magic.
  • Decorate-your-own cupcakes: a topping bar of orange icing, sprinkles, and sweet eyes. See our recipes guide for more treats.

Cooking together is quietly educational: measuring, counting, waiting for timers, and the pride of serving "their" creation to the family.

Indoor Games for Small Spaces

At-home games should respect furniture and neighbours. These favourites need little room and big imaginations:

  • Halloween bingo and memory matching with hand-drawn picture cards.
  • Sock ghost bowling: roll a soft ball at decorated paper cups; hallway approved.
  • Treasure hunt with picture clues: hide a "golden pumpkin" (a wrapped sweet) and give photo or drawing clues room to room.
  • Build a haunted house fort: blankets, cushions, torches, and stuffed-animal residents. Host tours for the family.
  • Monster drawing relay: each person draws one feature (eyes, horns, legs) before passing the paper on; unfold for the final reveal.

For bigger party-style games — including classroom sets — see our dedicated kids' games guide.

Toddler & Preschool Corner (Ages 2–4)

Little ones join Halloween best through gentle sensory play, not spooks. Try a "pumpkin wash" station (water, sponges, mini pumpkins), scoop-and-pour bowls of dry rice with hidden plastic spiders to find, big sticker scenes, a costume dress-up basket with mirrors, and a dance party to cheerful "monster" songs. Keep sessions short, lights warm, and every ghost friendly.

Screen Time That Earns Its Place

A family Halloween movie afternoon is a tradition worth keeping. Choose gentle, funny spooky films for young children, preview anything aimed at older kids, and pair the screening with popcorn "ghost hands" (popcorn in food-safe gloves with a sweet ring on one finger). A video call with dressed-up cousins — costume parade, best pumpkin vote — turns any home into a family party across cities and countries.

A Sample At-Home Halloween Day (Saturday, October 31, 2026)

  • Morning: pumpkin painting and handprint keepsakes; hang the bat garland.
  • Midday: kitchen club — mummy biscuits and fruit cups; set the lantern jars to charge near a window.
  • Afternoon: costume time, face paint (see our kids' makeup guide), and a photo session while light is good.
  • Late afternoon: indoor games rotation and treasure hunt.
  • Evening: lantern glow parade around the house, family movie, and a video-call costume parade with relatives.
  • Bedtime: one not-so-spooky story, makeup off, and pumpkins glowing as night-lights in the hallway (battery lights only).

Quick Decor Corners (30 Minutes, Whole House)

You do not need to decorate every room. Choose three corners and go deep: a door corner with the bat garland and one bold pumpkin, a window corner with lantern jars glowing at dusk, and a reading corner with cushions, a torch, and the haunted-house fort. Children can "own" one corner each — choosing its name, guarding it proudly, and giving tours to grandparents on video calls. A named corner ("Bat Cave," "Pumpkin HQ") somehow feels far more magical than the same decorations spread thinly through the house, and tidying up afterwards takes five minutes instead of an hour.

Safety & Budget Notes

Use battery lights instead of candles anywhere children play. Check craft materials for small parts with under-threes, supervise slime and water play, and label all food for allergies. Almost every activity here costs close to nothing: paper, paint, torches, and recycling-bin cardboard are the real craft kit. Spend only on the treats you would buy anyway — the magic is in doing it together.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can kids do at home for Halloween without trick-or-treating?

Decorate no-carve pumpkins, make lantern jars and paper garlands, bake simple treats, play a treasure hunt and indoor games, wear costumes for a video-call parade, and finish with a family movie. A full day of Halloween fits inside one home.

Which Halloween crafts suit toddlers?

Handprint ghosts and pumpkins, pumpkin washing stations, sticker scenes, and scoop-and-find sensory bowls. Keep everything large-sized, supervised, and friendly rather than spooky.

How can I celebrate Halloween in an apartment?

Focus on crafts, kitchen fun, costume photos, and small-space games like sock bowling and treasure hunts with picture clues. Lantern jars on the windowsill give neighbours a show without any noise.

What Halloween activities are screen-free?

All the crafts, kitchen activities, and indoor games in this guide are screen-free. Build one "analogue afternoon" around them and keep phones only for photos.

How do I include family who live far away?

Schedule a video-call costume parade, send handprint keepsakes by photo or post, and share your lantern parade on the family chat. Distance shrinks quickly when everyone is wearing a costume.

Conclusion

Halloween at home is not a backup plan — for many families it is the main event. Pick your crafts, plan one kitchen adventure, wear the costumes proudly, and let the lantern jars glow. Saturday, October 31, 2026 can be every bit as magical as any street in the world.
