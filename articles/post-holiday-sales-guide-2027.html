Post-Holiday Sales Guide 2027: Best Deals After Christmas

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 5 min read

Christmas ends, and the real bargain season begins. Retailers need to clear holiday inventory fast, which makes the days after Christmas one of the best buying windows of the entire year — if you know what to buy, when to buy it, and how to tell a genuine deal from a fake markdown. Here is your guide to the 2027 post-holiday sales.

When the Sales Actually Start

Do not wait until January. Most major retailers launch post-Christmas clearance on December 26 itself, with discounts typically starting at 30–50% and deepening to 70–90% through the first two weeks of January. The trade-off is classic: shop December 26–28 for the best selection, or wait until mid-January for the deepest discounts on whatever is left. Online sales often start Christmas evening. Mark your calendar: the sweet spot for most categories is the first week of January, when first-wave markdowns overlap with New Year sale events and inventory is still decent.

What Is Worth Buying After Christmas

Some categories genuinely bottom out after the holidays. Holiday decor, wrapping paper, and ornaments drop 70–90% — buy next year's supplies now and store them. Winter clothing and boots clear out as stores make room for spring lines. TVs and electronics see second-wave discounts after Black Friday leftovers did not sell. Toys hit clearance as retailers dump unsold stock — great for next year's birthdays. Gift sets (fragrance, skincare, food baskets) that missed the gifting window sell for a fraction of their price. Small appliances and bedding also see strong January "white sales." What is not worth it: anything you are buying just because it is cheap.

Spotting Real Deals vs. Fake Markdowns

Not every "70% off" is real. Retailers sometimes inflate the "original" price to make the discount look bigger. Protect yourself: check the price history with a tracker or a quick search of the product's typical selling price over the last few months. Compare the sale price across two or three retailers — genuine clearance is usually consistent everywhere. Read the fine print on "final sale" clearance items, which often cannot be returned. And set a rule before you shop: a deal is only a deal if it is something you already wanted or genuinely need. A 90% discount on something useless is still 100% wasted money.

Smart Strategies for Maximum Savings

Stack your savings. Use gift cards you received for Christmas on clearance items to double-dip. Check whether stores honor price adjustments — some will refund the difference if an item you bought in December drops further in January. Sign up for retailer newsletters in late December for subscriber-only clearance codes (unsubscribe in February). Shop store brands and open-box items for electronics. Buy next year's gifts now while wrapping and cards are pennies — keep a labeled bin so you actually use them. And set a hard budget before you browse clearance; the whole point is saving money, not finding new ways to spend it.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do after-Christmas sales start?

Most start December 26, with discounts of 30–50% that deepen to 70–90% through mid-January. Online clearance often begins Christmas evening. The best balance of selection and discount is usually the first week of January.

What should I buy during post-holiday sales?

Next year's holiday decor and wrapping (70–90% off), winter clothing, unsold electronics, clearance toys for future birthdays, and gift sets. Avoid buying things just because they are cheap — stock up on what you will actually use.

Are after-Christmas sales better than Black Friday?

For holiday decor, winter clothing, and toys — yes, clearance beats Black Friday. For TVs and major electronics, Black Friday usually wins on the headline items, though post-holiday is better for leftover models. They complement each other rather than compete.

Can I return clearance items?

It depends on the store and the item. Many retailers mark deep-clearance items "final sale" with no returns, while others keep normal policies. Always check the return terms before buying clearance — especially for electronics and clothing.
