DIY Halloween Costumes Under $20 (2026): Big Impact, Tiny Budget

Published October 17, 2026 · Updated October 17, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team · 4 min read

Great Halloween costumes are about ideas, not invoices. Some of the most complimented looks every year cost less than a pizza — built from closets, cardboard, and thrift-store luck.

This guide proves it: DIY Halloween costumes under $20 for 2026, each with a build list, a cost breakdown, and the one detail that sells the whole thing.

Closet-First Costumes ($0)

Start where the money already is: your wardrobe.

  • Tourist: loud shirt + sunglasses + camera + map. Cost: $0.
  • Black cat: all-black outfit + paper ears + eyeliner whiskers. Cost: $0.
  • Detective: trench or long coat + magnifying glass (toy) + notebook. Cost: $0–3.
  • Artist: beret + cardboard palette + paint smudge. Cost: $0–2.
  • CEO of naps: pyjamas + tie + briefcase + pillow. Cost: $0.
  • Error 404: white tee + handwritten sign. Cost: $0.

Cardboard Builds (Under $10)

Cardboard is Halloween’s secret weapon: free, light, and infinitely customisable.

  • Robot: box body + dryer-hose arms + foil details. Cost: ~$5 (foil + paint).
  • Playing card: large cardboard rectangle, painted suit. Cost: ~$3.
  • Lego brick: box + paper cups glued on top, single colour paint. Cost: ~$6.
  • Smartphone: tall box with a “screen” showing your costume. Cost: ~$4.
  • Dice: cube box, black dot stickers. Pair with a friend for the set. Cost: ~$4.

Tip: acrylic craft paint covers cardboard in one coat; hot glue beats tape for joints.

Thrift-Store Gold (Under $20)

  • Vintage detective: thrifted trench ($8–12) + fedora ($3–5).
  • 80s workout: neon top ($4) + leggings ($5) + sweatband ($2).
  • Mad scientist: white shirt ($4) + crazy wig ($5) + cardboard “formulas.”
  • Safari explorer: khaki shirt ($5) + hat ($4) + toy binoculars ($3).
  • Ghost of fashion past: the wildest thrifted outfit you can assemble ($10–15 total). Irony included free.

Shop thrift stores in early October — the good stuff goes fast. Weekday mornings have the best stock.

The $20 Budget Breakdown

Rule: spend on ONE thing. A $12 wig, a $10 face-paint kit, or a $15 statement piece carries a $0 outfit.

  • Face paint ($8–12): transforms any outfit into a character. Best ROI in Halloween.
  • One great wig ($10–15): colour and volume do the heavy lifting.
  • Thrifted statement piece ($8–15): a cape, a hat, a wild jacket.
  • Props ($3–8): a toy sword, a fake microphone, a cardboard sign.

Track spending as you go — the $20 challenge is half the fun. Share your build cost on social; budget DIY posts get huge engagement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really make a good costume for under $20?

Yes — the most complimented costumes are often the cleverest, not the priciest. One strong concept plus one good accessory beats a $80 bagged costume.

What is the cheapest Halloween costume ever?

Error 404 (white tee + sign) or tourist (closet clothes) cost literally $0 and always get laughs.

Where is the best place to thrift Halloween pieces?

Charity and thrift stores in early October. Go on weekday mornings, check the accessories wall first, and look beyond the “costume” section — regular clothes are the real gold.

How do I make cardboard costumes comfortable?

Cut armholes generously, wear soft clothes underneath, keep pieces light, and use ribbon straps instead of tape on skin. Ventilation holes are your friend.

Conclusion

Twenty dollars is plenty when the idea is strong. Build from your closet, raid the thrift store early, spend on one hero piece — and enjoy outshining costumes that cost five times more.
