Kids Halloween Party Games 2026: 20 Fun Activities

Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 13, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Kids do not need an elaborate Halloween party — they need games, preferably back-to-back, with prizes. For Halloween 2026 (Saturday, October 31), here are 20 kid-approved party games and activities: high-energy active games to burn off excitement, calm table games for winding down, craft stations that double as take-home favours, and prize ideas that cost almost nothing. Mix and match to fill any party from one hour to a full evening.

The kids-party formula: alternate active and calm games, keep each game under 15 minutes, and make sure every child wins something. Nobody remembers who won; everybody remembers who got nothing.

Active Games (Burn Off the Sugar Energy)

  1. Mummy wrap race: teams wrap one player head-to-toe in toilet paper. Fastest neat mummy wins. Hilarious photos guaranteed.
  2. Pin the hat on the witch: the Halloween version of pin the tail — blindfolded kids place a paper hat on a big witch poster.
  3. Monster freeze dance: play Halloween music; when it stops, everyone freezes in their scariest monster pose. Last to freeze is out.
  4. Pumpkin bowling: small pumpkins as bowling balls, plastic bottles decorated as ghosts as pins. Works indoors on carpet.
  5. Spider web obstacle course: crisscross crepe-paper "webs" across a hallway; kids crawl through without tearing them.
  6. Witch hat ring toss: toss rings (or glow bracelets) onto witch-hat cones. Simple, addictive, endlessly replayable.
  7. Ghost balloon pop relay: teams race to sit-pop ghost-faced balloons. Loud, chaotic, beloved.

Calm Table Games (The Wind-Down Zone)

  1. Halloween bingo: printed bingo cards with pumpkins, bats, and ghosts instead of numbers — a 20-card set keeps a whole classroom playing.
  2. Guess the mystery box: "peeled grape eyeballs", "spaghetti brains", "cauliflower zombie". Kids reach in blind and guess. Screams of delight guaranteed.
  3. Pumpkin tic-tac-toe: draw a grid on paper; mini pumpkins versus mini gourds as X and O.
  4. Halloween pictionary: teams draw ghosts, witches, and vampires against the clock. Terrible drawings are the whole point.
  5. Candy corn guessing jar: fill a jar, closest guess wins the jar. Runs itself all party long.
  6. Story circle: each child adds one spooky sentence to a group story. Funny-spooky only — set the tone first so nobody gets scared.

Craft Stations (Activities That Become Favours)

  1. Decorate-your-own pumpkin: mini pumpkins with paint pens and stickers — no carving, no mess, and every child takes theirs home.
  2. Haunted house craft kits: wooden or foam haunted-house kits kids assemble and decorate. A craft table keeps early arrivals busy while everyone shows up.
  3. Foam sticker masks: DIY foam sticker sets become instant costume accessories. Kids wear their creations for the rest of the party.
  4. Slime lab: pre-measured slime kits with green food colouring and googly eyes. "Witch slime" and "monster snot" are peak kid humour.
  5. Trick-or-treat bag decorating: plain paper bags plus Halloween stickers and markers. Practical — they use the bags later that night.

Prize Ideas (Cheap, Cheerful, Fair)

  • Everybody wins: give participation prizes for every game, not just winner prizes. A small candy or sticker for playing keeps moods high.
  • Prize box: a decorated box of wrapped mini prizes — kids pick one after each game. The mystery is half the fun.
  • Costume awards: funniest, scariest, most creative, best homemade, best group. Certificates printed at home cost nothing and get kept.
  • Non-candy prizes: Halloween pencils, temporary tattoos, glow sticks, and mini notebooks. Great for the teal-pumpkin (allergy-friendly) crowd.
  • Grand prize: one bigger prize for the final game of the night — a craft kit or plush — builds excitement through the whole party.

Age-by-Age Tips

  • Ages 3–5: keep games to 5 minutes, avoid anything scary or competitive, lean on crafts and simple movement games like freeze dance.
  • Ages 6–8: the sweet spot for all 20 games above. They love teams, points, and mild spookiness.
  • Ages 9–12: add strategy — Halloween trivia, escape-room-style puzzle hunts, and "fear factor" taste tests. They want to feel grown-up.
  • Mixed ages: pair younger kids with older "buddies" for team games, and always offer a craft table as an opt-out for kids who need a break.

Party Flow: A Sample 2-Hour Plan

  • 0:00–0:20: arrivals + craft station (decorating bags or pumpkins) as kids trickle in.
  • 0:20–0:50: two active games (mummy wrap, pumpkin bowling).
  • 0:50–1:10: snack break + candy corn guessing jar reveal.
  • 1:10–1:40: calm games (bingo, mystery box).
  • 1:40–2:00: costume parade with awards + grand prize + take-home bags.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many games do I need for a kids Halloween party?

Plan one game per 15–20 minutes of party time, alternating active and calm games. A two-hour party needs about 6 games plus a craft station for arrivals — this list of 20 gives you plenty to choose from.

What are good non-candy prizes for Halloween games?

Halloween pencils, temporary tattoos, glow sticks, stickers, and mini notebooks. They suit the teal-pumpkin allergy-friendly approach and kids genuinely love picking from a prize box.

How do I keep games from getting too competitive?

Give participation prizes for every game, rotate teams so nobody "loses" twice, and keep games short. For young kids, frame everything as a team challenge rather than a contest.

What games work for mixed age groups?

Bingo, pumpkin decorating, mystery box guessing, and freeze dance all span ages 3–12. Pair younger children with older buddies for team games, and keep a craft table as an always-open opt-out.

How scary should kids party games be?

Funny-spooky, never genuinely frightening. Mystery boxes, silly monster poses, and joke tombstones delight kids; jump scares, dark rooms, and realistic gore can genuinely upset younger children.

Conclusion

Twenty games, one simple rule: every child plays, every child wins something. Alternate wild and calm, keep a craft table running, end with a costume parade — and you will be the parent whose party gets talked about until next October.
