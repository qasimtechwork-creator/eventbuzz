Holiday Baking Guide 2026: Cookies, Cakes & Treats

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 5 min read

Holiday baking is one of those traditions that looks like a lot of work and mostly is not — a few reliable recipes, a plan, and the right storage make the whole season feel effortless. Whether you are baking for cookie exchanges, gifts, or just the smell of cinnamon filling the house, this guide covers what to bake, when to bake it, and how to keep everything fresh until the big day.

The Essential Holiday Baking List

You do not need forty recipes. A strong holiday lineup is: one roll-out sugar cookie (for decorating with kids), one drop cookie like chocolate chip or snickerdoodle, one spiced cookie like gingerbread or molasses, one no-bake treat like truffles or peppermint bark, and one showpiece cake — a yule log, spiced Bundt, or sticky toffee pudding. This mix covers every craving, suits every skill level, and photographs beautifully together on a dessert table. Pick recipes you have made at least once before for anything you are serving guests; holidays are not the time to test-drive a finicky new cake.

Plan Your Baking Calendar

The secret to stress-free holiday baking is spreading it out. Two to three weeks before: bake sturdy cookies like biscotti, shortbread, and gingerbread — they keep for weeks and actually taste better after a few days. One week before: make cookie doughs and freeze them in logs or scooped balls; slice-and-bake straight from frozen adds only a minute or two of bake time. The day before: frost sugar cookies, dip truffles, and assemble anything fragile. Day of: bake only what must be served warm, like a sticky pudding or fresh dinner rolls. A digital kitchen scale pays for itself here — weighing flour and sugar is faster and far more consistent than cup measurements.

Storage That Keeps Everything Fresh

Good storage is the difference between day-one delicious and day-five disappointing. Crisp cookies stay crisp in tins at room temperature with a piece of bread tucked in (the bread goes stale so the cookies do not). Soft cookies need airtight containers — plastic or glass with sealing lids — also at room temperature, but never mixed with crisp cookies in the same tin or they all go soft. Frosted cookies need a single layer with parchment between layers. Cakes keep two to three days at room temperature under a dome, or freeze undecorated layers wrapped tightly for up to a month. Peppermint bark and truffles are happy in the fridge for two weeks.

Baking as Gifts: The Easy Win

Homemade treats are the gift that never feels cheap if you present them well. Pack mixed cookies in kraft boxes with parchment, tie with ribbon and a sprig of pine, and add a handwritten tag — it takes five minutes and looks like a boutique bakery. Mason jars layered with dry cookie mix (plus a recipe card) make great last-minute gifts. Truffles in a small tin punch far above their cost. One caution: always ask about nut and gluten allergies before gifting food, and label what you made. A beautiful box means nothing if someone cannot safely eat it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance can I bake holiday cookies?

Sturdy cookies like shortbread, biscotti, and gingerbread keep 2–3 weeks in airtight tins. Soft cookies are best within a week. Cookie dough freezes beautifully for up to 3 months — scoop or shape it first, then bake straight from frozen.

Why should I weigh ingredients instead of using cups?

Flour packed into a cup can vary by 30% or more, which is enough to ruin cookies. A kitchen scale gives the same result every time, is faster once you are used to it, and means fewer dishes to wash.

How do I keep decorated sugar cookies from spreading?

Chill the cut shapes for 15–20 minutes before baking, bake on a cool (not warm) tray, and make sure your oven is fully preheated. Recipes with more flour relative to butter also hold their shape better.

Can I freeze frosted cakes and cookies?

Unfrosted cake layers freeze best — wrap tightly in plastic and foil for up to 3 months, then frost after thawing. Buttercream-frosted cookies freeze well; royal icing can crack, so freeze sugar cookies plain and ice them later.
