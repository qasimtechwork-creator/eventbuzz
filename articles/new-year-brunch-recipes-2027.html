New Year Brunch Recipes 2027: Easy Ideas for January 1st

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 7 min read

The first meal of 2027 deserves better than leftover chips. A New Year brunch on Friday, January 1st is the gentlest way to welcome the year — good food, no rush, and the people you counted down with last night. The golden rule of January 1st cooking: make it ahead. Assemble everything on December 31, then bake, reheat, and enjoy.

Make-Ahead Stars

These dishes go into the fridge on New Year's Eve and into the oven on New Year's Day — zero morning effort required.

  • Overnight French toast casserole: Cubed brioche soaked in egg custard with cinnamon; bake 45 minutes and serve with maple syrup and berries.
  • Egg and sausage bake: A savory strata of eggs, bread, cheese, and breakfast sausage that feeds a crowd from one dish.
  • Chia pudding jars: Chia seeds, milk, and a little honey set overnight; top with mango or berries in the morning.
  • Marinated breakfast potatoes: Dice and season potatoes the night before; roast at high heat for crispy edges.

Sweet Brunch Favorites

January 1st is not the day for restraint. These sweet classics bring celebration energy to the table.

  • Lemon ricotta pancakes: Light, fluffy, and bright — ricotta makes the batter tender and the lemon keeps them fresh.
  • Cinnamon roll waffles: Press cinnamon roll dough in a waffle iron; the ridges caramelize the cinnamon sugar.
  • Berry baked oatmeal: Oats baked with mixed berries and a crumble topping — warm, filling, and easily made for a group.
  • Chocolate babka French toast: Thick slices of chocolate babka dipped in custard and griddled golden.

Savory and Recovery-Friendly

After a late night, guests crave salt, protein, and something warm. These deliver comfort without fuss.

  • Shakshuka: Eggs poached in spiced tomato sauce, served straight from the skillet with crusty bread.
  • Breakfast tacos: Scrambled eggs, black beans, avocado, and salsa in warm tortillas — endlessly customizable.
  • Smoked salmon bagel board: Bagels, cream cheese, salmon, capers, and red onion arranged on a board for grazing.
  • Miso soup with noodles: A light, hydrating bowl that works wonders the morning after.

Drinks for the Table

Brunch drinks should be easy to make in batches and kind to tired guests.

  • Classic mimosas: Chilled prosecco with fresh orange juice — keep the ratio half and half.
  • Bloody Mary bar: A pitcher of mix plus celery, pickles, olives, and hot sauce so guests build their own.
  • Citrus infused water: Big jugs of water with orange, lemon, and mint for steady hydration.
  • Slow-brew coffee: Start a large pot or cold brew the night before; tired guests will thank you first.

Hosting Tips for January 1st

Keep the guest list small and the schedule loose — brunch at 11:00 or noon beats an early call time after a late night. Use disposable-but-pretty plates to skip the dish mountain, set out food buffet-style so people graze, and keep one dish warming in the oven for late arrivals. Ask about dietary needs in advance: a fruit platter, a dairy-free milk option for coffee, and one gluten-free dish cover most bases without extra cooking. Most importantly, give yourself permission to keep it simple — the point of January 1st brunch is starting the year together, not performing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best make-ahead New Year brunch dish?

An overnight French toast casserole or an egg and sausage bake. Assemble either on December 31, refrigerate overnight, and bake on January 1st morning — no cooking while half asleep.

What time should I host New Year brunch?

11:00 AM or noon works best after a late New Year's Eve. It gives guests time to recover and gives you time to bake the make-ahead dishes.

What drinks should I serve at New Year brunch?

Mimosas, a Bloody Mary bar, plenty of coffee, and citrus water for hydration. Batch everything so you are not playing bartender all morning.

How do I handle dietary restrictions at brunch?

Ask guests in advance, then cover the basics: a fruit platter, a dairy-free milk for coffee, and one gluten-free dish. Buffet-style serving lets everyone build a plate that works for them.
