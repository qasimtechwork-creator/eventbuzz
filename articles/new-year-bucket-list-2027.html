New Year Bucket List 2027: 50 Ideas to Make This Your Best Year

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 8 min read

A bucket list is a resolution list with better PR — it is about experiences, not obligations. Instead of "exercise more," try "hike a trail I have never walked." This 2027 bucket list gives you 50 ideas across travel, skills, money, relationships, and pure fun. Pick 10 to 15 that genuinely excite you, schedule at least one per month, and watch the year fill up with stories.

Travel Adventures

  • Visit a country you have never been to: Even a short trip somewhere new rewires your perspective.
  • Take a road trip with no fixed destination: Pick a direction, drive, and stop wherever looks interesting.
  • Sleep under the stars: A camping night in a dark-sky area — check the moon phase for the best view.
  • Watch sunrise from a mountaintop: Pick an accessible peak and time the hike for dawn.
  • Explore your own city like a tourist: Book a walking tour, visit the museum you keep skipping, eat somewhere new.
  • Take a solo day trip: A train ride, a beach, a book — solitude counts as an adventure.
  • Swim in the ocean at sunrise: Cold, bracing, unforgettable.
  • Visit a famous landmark you have always seen in photos: This is the year to stand in front of it.

Skills to Learn

  • Cook one cuisine from scratch: Pick a cuisine — Thai, Italian, Japanese — and master five dishes.
  • Learn 100 phrases in a new language: Enough for a trip; apps make daily practice easy.
  • Take a photography course: Learn to shoot manual and finally understand your camera.
  • Learn to swim properly: If you can only doggy-paddle, fix that this year.
  • Pick up an instrument: Guitar, ukulele, piano — 15 minutes a day adds up fast.
  • Learn basic car or bike maintenance: Change a tire, check fluids, fix a flat.
  • Master one party trick: A card trick, a cocktail, a signature dish — something fun to show off.
  • Take a first-aid course: Genuinely useful and surprisingly empowering.

Money and Career Moves

  • Build a three-month emergency fund: The single most calming financial goal there is.
  • Start a side project that earns its first dollar: Freelancing, a small shop, a digital product — prove the concept.
  • Negotiate a raise or a better rate: Prepare your case and ask this year.
  • Pay off one debt completely: Pick the smallest balance and kill it.
  • Invest your first amount: Even a small, regular contribution starts the habit.
  • Update your portfolio or resume: Capture this year's wins while they are fresh.
  • Attend one industry event: Conferences and meetups open doors that emails cannot.
  • Save for one big dream purchase: Name it, price it, and automate the saving.

Relationships and Memories

  • Write letters to five people who matter: Handwritten, mailed, real paper.
  • Plan a reunion with old friends: Be the one who organizes it — it will not happen otherwise.
  • Have a weekly device-free dinner: Phones in a drawer, conversation on the table.
  • Interview a grandparent or elder: Record their stories; you will treasure the recording forever.
  • Volunteer for one cause: A beach cleanup, a food bank shift, a mentorship hour.
  • Host a dinner party from scratch: Full menu, real table setting, the works.
  • Forgive someone (and mean it): The lightest feeling of the year.
  • Take a photo with every friend you see this year: The album becomes a treasure.

Pure Fun and Firsts

  • Try a food you have always avoided: Sushi, oysters, durian — expand the palate.
  • Go to a concert of a genre you do not know: Jazz, opera, metal — surprise yourself.
  • Take a dance class: Salsa, swing, hip-hop — pick one and commit to a month.
  • Do a digital detox weekend: 48 hours, no social media, no news. Notice what changes.
  • Run or walk a 5K event: The medal is a great souvenir of the year.
  • Stargaze during a meteor shower: Check the calendar for the Perseids in August or the Geminids in December.
  • Learn a magic trick and perform it: Then teach it to a kid.
  • Watch every sunrise for one week: A small, strange, wonderful challenge.
  • Build something with your hands: Furniture, a garden bed, a model — make a thing.
  • Do one thing that scares you: Public speaking, a cold plunge, a difficult conversation — growth lives there.
  • Keep a one-line-a-day journal all year: On December 31, 2027, read the whole year back.
  • Print your favorite photos: A physical album beats a camera roll every time.
  • Donate blood: Quick, free, and genuinely saves lives.
  • Plant a tree: In 2027, watch it grow all year.
  • Say yes to one spontaneous invitation: The unplanned yes often becomes the best story.
  • End the year with a tradition: Pick one from this list and repeat it every year — that is how traditions start.

How to use this list: Copy the ideas that excite you into your own notes, add five that are personal to you, and put dates next to at least six of them. A bucket list with dates becomes a plan; a plan becomes a year you will actually remember.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many items should be on my 2027 bucket list?

Ten to fifteen is the practical sweet spot — enough variety for a full year, few enough to actually complete. Quality of experience beats quantity of checkboxes.

What is the difference between a bucket list and resolutions?

Resolutions are usually obligations (lose weight, save money); bucket lists are experiences (visit a new country, learn an instrument). The experience framing tends to be more motivating and more fun.

How do I make sure I actually complete my bucket list?

Put dates on at least half the items, schedule one per month, and review the list quarterly. Sharing it with a friend or partner adds gentle accountability.

Can I add personal items to this bucket list?

Yes — the best bucket lists are personal. Use these 50 ideas as a starting point, then add five to ten items that only matter to you: places from your childhood, skills from your dreams, people you miss.
