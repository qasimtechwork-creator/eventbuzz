Eid al-Fitr 2027 Date: When Is Eid and How the Moon Sighting Works

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

The honest answer to 'when is Eid 2027?' has two parts: the expected date in calendars and the confirmed date nobody can give until the Shawwal crescent is seen. This guide explains both — the expected Tuesday 9 to Wednesday 10 March 2027 window, how moon sighting works, and why dates differ by country — so you can plan without guessing.

Quick answer: Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected on Tuesday, March 9 or Wednesday, March 10, 2027. The exact day is confirmed only by the sighting of the Shawwal moon on Chand Raat, the night before Eid, so keep both days pencilled in until the official announcement.

When Is Eid al-Fitr 2027 Expected?

Eid al-Fitr is celebrated on 1 Shawwal, the first day of the tenth month of the Islamic lunar calendar, immediately after Ramadan ends. Because a lunar month lasts either 29 or 30 days, everything depends on how Ramadan completes:

  • If the Shawwal crescent is sighted on the evening of the 29th day of Ramadan, the next morning is Eid. For 2027, that points to Tuesday, March 9, 2027.
  • If the crescent is not sighted that evening, Ramadan completes a full 30 days and Eid falls a day later, on Wednesday, March 10, 2027.

Both dates are genuine possibilities, which is why you will see "expected" printed next to every Eid date in every calendar. The calendar gives you the window; the sky gives you the day.

How the Moon Sighting Works

The Islamic calendar is lunar, which means each month begins with the birth of a new crescent moon, called the hilal. A lunar year is about 354 days, roughly 11 days shorter than the solar year, which is why Ramadan and Eid move earlier through the seasons over the years.

On the evening of the 29th of Ramadan, just after sunset, moon sighting committees, religious scholars and ordinary families all watch the western horizon. The young crescent is thin and faint, visible for only a short window after sunset, so clear skies matter. In Pakistan, the Central Ruet-e-Hilal Committee collects testimony from across the country before announcing whether the moon has been sighted. If credible sightings are confirmed, Eid is declared for the next morning. If not, fasting continues for one more day and the announcement comes the following night instead.

Modern telescopes and astronomical calculations can predict where the crescent should be, and many communities use those calculations to support the announcement. But in much of the Muslim world, the final word still belongs to the sighted moon and the official declaration, which is why families stay close to the news on that last evening of Ramadan.

What Is Chand Raat?

Chand Raat, literally "the night of the moon," is the evening the Shawwal crescent is sighted and Eid is confirmed for the next morning. It is a celebration in its own right. Markets stay open late, bangle and mehndi stalls fill up, bakeries sell out, and the congratulatory messages start flying before the night is over. Many women and girls apply their henna on Chand Raat so the colour deepens beautifully by Eid morning; our Eid mehndi designs guide has simple patterns you can try that night. Once the announcement lands, the cooking, ironing and phone calls begin in earnest.

Why Eid Dates Can Differ by Country

It is common for one country to celebrate Eid a day before or after its neighbour, and nobody has made a mistake when that happens. A few simple reasons explain it:

  • Local sighting: the crescent may be visible in one region on an evening when clouds hide it in another.
  • Different methods: some countries rely on naked-eye sighting, others accept telescope sightings or astronomical calculation, and some follow the announcement of another country.
  • Time zones: sunset, and therefore the sighting window, arrives at different times across the world.

The practical rule is simple: celebrate with your local community and follow your own country's official announcement for prayers, holidays and greetings.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Eid al-Fitr 2027?

Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected on Tuesday, March 9 or Wednesday, March 10, 2027. The exact day is confirmed by the sighting of the Shawwal crescent on the last evening of Ramadan.

What decides the exact day of Eid?

The sighting of the new crescent moon of Shawwal. If it is seen on the evening of the 29th of Ramadan, Eid is the next morning; if not, Ramadan completes 30 days and Eid comes a day later.

What is Chand Raat?

Chand Raat is the night the Shawwal moon is sighted and Eid is confirmed for the next day. Families celebrate with last-minute shopping, mehndi, sweets and the first round of Eid greetings.

Why do some countries celebrate Eid on a different day?

Because moon sighting is local. Weather, time zones and different sighting methods mean one country may confirm the crescent a day earlier or later than another.

Can the Eid date be confirmed in advance?

Calendars can predict the expected window very accurately, but in countries that follow sighting, official confirmation only comes on Chand Raat itself. Plan for both possible days.

Conclusion

Eid al-Fitr 2027 will arrive on Tuesday, March 9 or Wednesday, March 10, and the sighting of one thin crescent will decide which. Until Chand Raat brings the announcement, prepare for both days, enjoy the last evenings of Ramadan, and keep your greetings ready.
