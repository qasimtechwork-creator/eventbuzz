15 Christmas Side Dishes 2026: Delicious Holiday Accompaniments

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

Side dishes are the supporting cast that makes Christmas dinner a feast. The turkey or ham gets the glory, but guests remember the creamy mashed potatoes, the crispy-edged stuffing, and the roasted vegetables they went back for seconds of. The trick is choosing sides that can mostly be made ahead and that reheat well, so your oven is free for the main event. Here are 15 proven Christmas side dishes for 2026.

Classic Potato Sides

Creamy mashed potatoes are non-negotiable in many households. Use Yukon Gold potatoes, warm your milk and butter before mashing, and never use a food processor, which turns them gummy. A ricer or hand masher gives the silkiest texture. For something richer, scalloped potato gratin layers thin potato slices with cream, garlic, and Gruyère, and it reheats beautifully.

Roasted garlic mashed potatoes add depth with a whole head of roasted garlic mashed in, while twice-baked potatoes stuffed with cheddar, bacon, and chives can be assembled a day ahead and finished in the oven while the roast rests.

Roasted and Glazed Vegetables

The oven is already hot, so put it to work. Honey-glazed carrots roasted with thyme until caramelized at the edges are sweet, simple, and gorgeous. Roasted Brussels sprouts with bacon and balsamic turn skeptics into fans; roast them cut-side down at 425°F for maximum crispiness. Parmesan-roasted cauliflower and balsamic roasted root vegetables with parsnips and sweet potatoes round out the tray.

Roast vegetables at high heat on a single layer with space between pieces. Crowded pans steam instead of caramelize, which is the number one reason roasted vegetables disappoint.

Casseroles and Comfort Sides

Casseroles are the ultimate make-ahead Christmas sides. Green bean casserole with homemade mushroom sauce and crispy fried onions beats the canned version by miles. Cornbread stuffing with sausage and sage, sweet potato casserole with a pecan streusel topping, and mac and cheese with a sharp cheddar béchamel all go from fridge to oven in one dish.

Creamed spinach with nutmeg and Parmesan is the steakhouse side that belongs on a Christmas table, and it comes together in 15 minutes on the stovetop while the oven is busy with the main.

Breads and Fresh Sides

Every Christmas dinner needs something to mop up gravy. Warm dinner rolls with flaky salt and butter are the classic, and frozen par-baked rolls are a fine shortcut. Yorkshire pudding is mandatory with prime rib and impressive with any roast. For freshness, a winter pear and walnut salad or shaved Brussels sprout salad with cranberries cuts through the richness, and homemade cranberry sauce with orange zest belongs on every plate.

Planning and Timing Tips

Choose three to five sides and assign each a cooking method so you are not fighting for oven space. A winning spread mixes one stovetop dish, one make-ahead casserole, one roasted vegetable, one bread, and one cold or room-temperature item. Practical tips:

  • Make ahead: Casseroles, stuffing, cranberry sauce, and mashed potatoes can be assembled or fully cooked 1 to 2 days early.
  • Reheat smart: Mashed potatoes reheat with a splash of warm milk; casseroles reheat covered at 350°F for 20 to 30 minutes.
  • Use the roast's rest: When the turkey or prime rib comes out, you have 30 to 45 minutes of free oven time. Roast vegetables and finish casseroles then.
  • Keep warm: A slow cooker on warm holds mashed potatoes and gravy perfectly for an hour.
  • Balance the plate: Aim for color contrast: green beans, orange carrots, red cranberries against the creamy potatoes.

Write your side-dish timeline next to your main-dish timeline, and Christmas dinner day becomes a calm sequence of tasks instead of a juggling act.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many side dishes should I make for Christmas?

Three to five sides is the sweet spot for most gatherings: one starch, one or two vegetables, one bread, and cranberry sauce. More than five creates oven traffic jams.

Which Christmas sides can be made ahead?

Casseroles, stuffing, cranberry sauce, pie, and mashed potatoes can be made or assembled 1 to 2 days ahead. Roasted vegetables are best done fresh on the day.

How do I keep mashed potatoes warm?

Hold them in a slow cooker on the warm setting with a little extra butter on top, or in a covered bowl over a pot of simmering water. Stir in warm milk when serving.

What is the difference between stuffing and dressing?

Stuffing is cooked inside the bird; dressing is baked separately in a dish. Dressing is safer and gets crispier edges, which is why most cooks prefer it.

How do I reheat Christmas side dishes?

Reheat casseroles covered at 350°F for 20 to 30 minutes, refresh mashed potatoes with warm milk, and re-crisp roasted vegetables at 425°F for 5 to 10 minutes.
