Holiday Travel Guide 2026: Tips for Christmas Trips

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 5 min read

Christmas travel is magical and stressful in equal measure. Airports are packed, roads are icy, and every delay feels worse when family is waiting at the other end. The good news is that most holiday travel pain is predictable, which means it is also preventable. Whether you are flying across the country or driving two towns over, a little planning turns a chaotic trip into part of the celebration. Here is your complete guide for Christmas travel in 2026.

When to Book Holiday Flights

The single biggest saver is booking early. For Christmas travel, fares generally climb as December approaches, so buying tickets in September or October is usually cheaper than waiting. Midweek flights are consistently easier than weekend ones: flying out on Christmas Eve or Christmas Day itself often costs less and means quieter airports. If your dates are flexible, compare a day earlier or later in both directions; shifting by one day can change the price dramatically. Set a fare alert early and buy when the price dips rather than trying to time the absolute bottom.

Driving Instead of Flying

For trips under six or seven hours, driving often beats flying once you count airport time, security lines and baggage claim. Winter driving needs preparation: check tire tread, keep an emergency kit with blankets, water, a phone charger and snacks, and fill up before remote stretches. Leave early in the day so you arrive before dark, and check road conditions along your route the night before. If snow is forecast, build in a buffer day rather than gambling on a tight arrival schedule.

Packing Smart for Winter Travel

Winter clothes are bulky, so pack with a plan. Wear your heaviest coat and boots on the plane instead of stuffing them in a bag. Roll clothes to save space and use packing cubes to keep outfits organized. Ship bulky gifts ahead of time instead of flying with them: it saves luggage space and avoids the risk of a wrapped gift being unwrapped during a bag check. Keep medications, chargers, a change of clothes and any Christmas-morning essentials in your carry-on, so a delayed suitcase does not ruin the holiday.

Surviving Travel Delays

Delays are the norm in late December, so travel like you expect them. Download your airline's app for faster rebooking, and know your flight's backup options before you leave for the airport. Pack snacks and entertainment for kids, because a two-hour delay with nothing to do feels like six. Travel insurance is worth considering for expensive or nonrefundable trips, but read the fine print so you know exactly what counts as a covered reason. Most importantly, build slack into your plans: arriving a day early costs less stress than sprinting through a connection on Christmas Eve.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest day to fly around Christmas?

Christmas Day itself and New Year's Day are usually among the cheapest travel days, with Christmas Eve close behind. The most expensive days are the weekends immediately before and after Christmas.

Can I bring wrapped gifts through airport security?

Yes, but wrapped gifts may be unwrapped for inspection if they need a closer look. It is safer to carry gifts unwrapped and wrap them after you arrive, or ship them ahead of time.

Is travel insurance worth it for Christmas trips?

For expensive, nonrefundable bookings it can be. Compare the cost of the policy against what you would lose if you had to cancel, and check exactly which situations are covered before you buy.

How early should I arrive at the airport during the holidays?

Two hours for domestic flights and three for international is the standard advice, and during the Christmas rush the upper end of that is safer. Morning flights also tend to have fewer cascading delays.
