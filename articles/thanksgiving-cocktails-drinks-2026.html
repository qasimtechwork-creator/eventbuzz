Thanksgiving Cocktails & Drinks 2026: 20 Festive Recipes

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 6 min read

A thoughtful drink lineup makes Thanksgiving feel like an occasion from the moment guests walk in. You do not need a full bar — one signature cocktail, a big-batch option, a few non-alcoholic choices, and the right wine cover every guest. These 20 Thanksgiving cocktails and drinks lean into fall flavors like apple, cranberry, cinnamon, and maple, with plenty of make-ahead and self-serve options so you are not playing bartender all day.

Signature Fall Cocktails

  • Apple cider margarita: Tequila, apple cider, lime juice, and a cinnamon-sugar rim. The unofficial cocktail of fall.
  • Cranberry bourbon smash: Bourbon muddled with fresh cranberries, maple syrup, and lemon, topped with ginger beer.
  • Pumpkin spice white Russian: Vodka, coffee liqueur, and cream with a pinch of pumpkin pie spice.
  • Maple old fashioned: The classic old fashioned sweetened with real maple syrup instead of sugar. Made for November.
  • Pear and ginger sparkler: Pear nectar, vodka, and ginger beer over ice with a rosemary sprig.
  • Spiced rum cider: Warm or cold apple cider with spiced rum and a cinnamon stick stirrer.

Big-Batch Punch & Sangria

For a crowd, batch it. These scale easily and let guests serve themselves:

  • Thanksgiving punch: Cranberry juice, apple cider, ginger ale, and sliced oranges and cranberries in a punch bowl. Add vodka or bourbon on the side for those who want it.
  • Apple cider sangria: White wine, apple cider, brandy, and sliced apples and pears, chilled overnight so the flavors meld.
  • Cranberry orange sangria: Red wine with cranberry juice, orange liqueur, and fresh cranberries and orange wheels.
  • Mulled wine: Red wine simmered with cinnamon, cloves, star anise, and orange. Keep warm in a slow cooker all evening.
  • Slow cooker mulled cider (spiked or not): Apple cider with mulling spices, kept warm for hours. Offer bourbon or rum alongside for a spiked version.

Non-Alcoholic Crowd-Pleasers

Always have appealing options for kids, designated drivers, and non-drinkers — not just soda:

  • Sparkling cranberry spritz: Cranberry juice, sparkling water, lime, and frozen cranberries. Festive and refreshing.
  • Caramel apple mocktail: Apple cider with caramel syrup and a pinch of cinnamon, served over ice with an apple slice.
  • Pomegranate lemonade: Lemonade mixed with pomegranate juice and sparkling water. Gorgeous color, easy to batch.
  • Hot chocolate bar: With whipped cream, marshmallows, caramel drizzle, and crushed peppermint. A hit with all ages.

Wine & Beer Pairings

  • Pinot noir: The classic Thanksgiving red — light enough for turkey, fruity enough for cranberry sauce.
  • Riesling or gewürztraminer: Slightly sweet whites that handle the sweet-savory spread beautifully.
  • Unoaked chardonnay: Crisp and food-friendly without the heavy oak that fights with the meal.
  • Sparkling wine: Cava or prosecco works from appetizers through dessert and feels celebratory.
  • Fall beers: A brown ale or amber ale pairs well with roasted flavors; keep a few on hand for beer drinkers.

Setting Up a Self-Serve Drink Station

  • Pick one signature drink: Batch a single cocktail and label it. Guests love a named house drink, and you avoid a dozen custom orders.
  • Ice, ice, ice: Buy more than you think you need. A 10-pound bag per 8 to 10 guests is a safe estimate for a long afternoon.
  • Garnish tray: Cinnamon sticks, orange wheels, fresh cranberries, rosemary sprigs, and apple slices make simple drinks look intentional.
  • Label everything: Small tent cards for each drink — especially noting which are non-alcoholic — save you from repeating yourself all day.
  • Glassware math: Plan on 2 to 3 glasses per guest if you are not running the dishwasher mid-party. Rentals are cheap for big crowds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good signature cocktail for Thanksgiving?

An apple cider margarita or cranberry bourbon smash are both excellent choices — they use fall flavors, batch well, and appeal to a wide range of tastes. Pick one, make a big batch, and set it out as the house drink.

How much alcohol do I need for a Thanksgiving party?

A practical rule is one bottle of wine per 2 to 3 wine drinkers, plus ingredients for one batched cocktail. Thanksgiving is a long, food-heavy day, so people generally drink less than at an evening party. It is better to have a little extra than to run out.

What non-alcoholic drinks should I serve at Thanksgiving?

Offer at least two appealing options beyond soda: a sparkling cranberry spritz or pomegranate lemonade for all ages, plus warm apple cider or a hot chocolate bar. Label them clearly so non-drinkers feel included.

Can I make Thanksgiving cocktails ahead of time?

Yes. Sangrias actually improve overnight in the fridge. Punch bases (without carbonation or ice) can be mixed a day ahead — add sparkling elements and ice just before serving. Mulled wine and cider can be kept warm in a slow cooker for hours.
