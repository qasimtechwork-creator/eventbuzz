Pakistan Day Speech in English for Students (23 March)

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Every year on 23 March, schools across Pakistan fill with flags, songs and speeches. If you have been chosen to speak, this page is for you: a short two minute speech, a fuller five minute speech, and a simple ten line version for young children, all in easy English. Pakistan Day 2027 falls on Tuesday, 23 March 2027, so there is plenty of time to practise. Read the speech aloud, change a line or two into your own voice, and remember that sincere delivery matters more than difficult words.

Quick answer: Pakistan Day is celebrated on 23 March because the Lahore Resolution was passed on 23 March 1940 at Minto Park, Lahore, and because Pakistan became the world’s first Islamic republic on 23 March 1956. Use the two minute speech below for assemblies and the ten line version for young kids.

How to Use These Speeches

The two minute speech suits most assemblies. The five minute speech is better for main stage events, competitions and older students. The ten line version uses very simple words for young children, who can learn it in a day or two. Teachers may shorten any speech by removing a paragraph; each is built so sections lift out cleanly. All three share the same true story: a peaceful, lawful demand for a homeland, made with words, unity and faith.

Two Minute Speech

Respected principal, honourable teachers and my dear friends, Assalam o Alaikum. Today we have gathered to celebrate Pakistan Day, the day that changed our history forever. On 23 March 1940, at Minto Park in Lahore, the All India Muslim League passed the famous Lahore Resolution. The session was presided over by our great leader, Quaid e Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah, and the resolution was presented by A. K. Fazlul Huq. That day, the Muslims of the subcontinent clearly demanded a separate homeland where they could live freely according to their own faith, culture and values.

Only seven years later, on 14 August 1947, that dream became Pakistan. And there is a second reason we honour this date: on 23 March 1956, Pakistan adopted its first constitution and became the world’s first Islamic republic. So 23 March reminds us of both our demand and our achievement. My friends, a homeland was given to us through the hard work and sacrifices of our elders. Protecting it, studying honestly, respecting the law and serving others is now our duty. Let us promise today that we will keep Pakistan clean, united and strong. Pakistan Zindabad. Thank you.

Five Minute Speech

Respected principal, respected teachers and my dear fellow students, Assalam o Alaikum. Today I speak about one of the most important days in our history, Pakistan Day, celebrated every year on 23 March. To understand it, we travel back to 1940, when the Muslims of British India feared that in a united India their rights, faith and identity would not be protected.

From 22 to 24 March 1940, the All India Muslim League held its historic session at Minto Park in Lahore, presided over by Quaid e Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah. On 23 March 1940, A. K. Fazlul Huq presented the Lahore Resolution, demanding that the Muslim majority regions be grouped into independent states where Muslims could live with dignity and freedom.

That day gave our struggle a clear destination. Students just like us carried the message from city to village, peacefully and lawfully, with words, unity and faith. Their struggle succeeded, and on 14 August 1947 Pakistan appeared on the map of the world.

The date carries one more honour: on 23 March 1956, Pakistan became the world’s first Islamic republic. That is why we mark the day with parades, flag hoisting and school programmes. But ceremonies alone are not enough. Let us promise to study honestly, keep our surroundings clean, and respect every fellow Pakistani. Pakistan Zindabad. Thank you.

Simple Ten Line Speech for Young Kids

  1. Assalam o Alaikum, respected teachers and dear friends.
  2. Today I will speak about Pakistan Day.
  3. We celebrate Pakistan Day on 23 March every year.
  4. On 23 March 1940, our leaders gathered at Minto Park in Lahore.
  5. Quaid e Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah led that great meeting.
  6. Our elders demanded a separate homeland for Muslims.
  7. Because of their hard work, Pakistan was created on 14 August 1947.
  8. Pakistan is our home, and we love our green flag.
  9. I will study hard and keep my country clean.
  10. Thank you, and Pakistan Zindabad.

Delivery Tips for Students

Practise out loud, not in your head. Read slowly to a family member or record yourself once; you will hear which lines feel too fast. Learn the ideas, not only the words, so if you forget a line you can continue without panic. Speak a little slower than conversation, pause after the dates, and look up at your audience at the end of each paragraph. Stand straight and keep your voice strong on the first and last lines. Most of all, mean what you say: a short speech with true feeling beats a long one read without heart.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Pakistan Day celebrated on 23 March?

Because the Lahore Resolution was passed on 23 March 1940 at Minto Park, Lahore, demanding a separate homeland for Muslims, and because Pakistan became the world’s first Islamic republic on 23 March 1956.

Who presented the Lahore Resolution?

A. K. Fazlul Huq presented the resolution during the All India Muslim League session at Minto Park, Lahore. The session was presided over by Quaid e Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah.

When is Pakistan Day 2027?

Pakistan Day 2027 falls on Tuesday, 23 March 2027. Schools and offices across Pakistan mark it with ceremonies, parades and special programmes.

How long should a school speech be?

Two minutes suits most assemblies, which is roughly 250 to 300 spoken words. Five minutes suits competitions and main events. Young children do best with the simple ten line version.

Can I change these speeches for my school?

Yes. These speeches are free for students and schools to use and adapt. Add your school name or shorten a paragraph.

Conclusion

Pakistan Day is hope achieved through unity, faith and discipline. Choose your speech, practise with heart, and deliver it proudly on 23 March 2027.
