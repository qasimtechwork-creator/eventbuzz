Classic Mulled Wine Recipe 2026: Warm Spiced Holiday Drink

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

Few things say Christmas quite like the scent of mulled wine drifting through the house. Warm, spiced, and ruby-red, this centuries-old holiday drink turns a simple bottle of red wine into something magical with nothing more than orange, cinnamon, and patience. It is forgiving to make, easy to scale for a party, and your home will smell incredible for hours. Here is everything you need to make a classic batch this Christmas 2026.

Choosing the Right Wine

Do not reach for an expensive bottle: heat and spice will mask the subtleties you pay for. A dry, fruity, medium-bodied red works best, such as a Merlot, Grenache, or Tempranillo in the budget-friendly range. Avoid anything heavily oaked or very tannic, since tannins become harsh when heated. Boxed wine is genuinely fine here and economical for a party batch. White wine fans can swap in a dry white like Pinot Grigio for a lighter "mulled white wine" with the same spices plus a squeeze of lemon.

The Essential Spice Blend

The classic mulled wine spice mix is simple and hard to get wrong. For each 750ml bottle of wine, use:

  • 1 orange, sliced into rounds, plus 2 strips of peel
  • 3 cinnamon sticks
  • 4 whole cloves
  • 2 star anise
  • 3 cardamom pods, lightly crushed
  • 1 bay leaf (optional, adds depth)
  • 1/4 cup honey or brown sugar, adjusted to taste
  • 1/4 cup brandy or orange liqueur, added at the end (optional)

Whole spices are better than ground here: ground spices cloud the wine and leave sediment. A small cheesecloth sachet keeps everything together if you want crystal-clear pours.

Stovetop Classic Mulled Wine Recipe

Combine the wine, orange slices and peel, spices, and honey in a heavy-bottomed pot over medium-low heat. Stir until the honey dissolves, then reduce the heat to the lowest setting so the mixture gently steams without ever boiling. Let it infuse for 20 to 30 minutes, tasting once at the 15-minute mark to adjust sweetness. Strain into heatproof glasses or mugs, stir in the brandy if using, and garnish each glass with a fresh orange slice and a cinnamon stick. One bottle serves 4 to 5 generous mugs. Never let it boil: boiling cooks off the alcohol and turns the wine bitter and flat.

Slow-Cooker Method for Parties

For a Christmas gathering, the slow cooker is unbeatable: it holds the wine at serving temperature for hours with zero attention. Scale the recipe to 3 bottles of wine with tripled spices, pour everything into the cooker, and heat on low for 1 hour until warm, then switch to the "warm" setting for serving. Guests can help themselves with a ladle straight from the pot. Add the orange liqueur just before guests arrive rather than at the start so its flavor stays bright. Set out the mugs, garnish tray, and a small sign, and your drinks station runs itself.

Serving and Storing Tips

Serve mulled wine in heatproof glass mugs or thick ceramic cups so guests can admire the color. Leftover mulled wine keeps well: strain out the spices and fruit, refrigerate for up to 3 days, and reheat gently on the stove without boiling. The spices can technically be reused once for a weaker second batch, but for best flavor, start fresh. If your batch tastes flat, a splash of orange juice or an extra spoonful of honey usually fixes it. And if it tastes bitter, the culprit is almost always too much heat or too many cloves, so go easy on both next time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best red wine for mulled wine?

A dry, fruity, medium-bodied red like Merlot, Grenache, or Tempranillo. Avoid expensive or heavily oaked bottles since heat and spices mask their nuances.

Can I make mulled wine non-alcoholic?

Yes. Replace the wine with a mix of grape juice and cranberry juice and simmer with the same spices. Add a splash of orange juice at the end for brightness.

How long can I keep mulled wine warm?

In a slow cooker on the warm setting, it holds well for 3 to 4 hours. After that the fruit breaks down and the flavors dull, so make a fresh batch for long events.

Why is my mulled wine bitter?

The two usual causes are letting it boil and using too many cloves. Keep the heat at a gentle steam and stick to about 4 cloves per bottle.

Can I reuse the spices for another batch?

You can once, but the flavor will be noticeably weaker. For the best-tasting mulled wine, use fresh spices for each new batch.
