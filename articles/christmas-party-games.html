Christmas Party Games to Play: Fun Ideas for Adults & Kids

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Food brings people to a Christmas party; games are what they talk about on the way home. The secret is not buying anything. It is choosing games with rules you can explain in one sentence and stakes so low that even the shyest guest joins in by round two. Every game below needs nothing more than paper, pens, wrapped gifts, or things already in your kitchen drawers.

Quick answer: Short on time? Run a White Elephant gift exchange for adults, Christmas charades for mixed ages, and the candy cane relay for kids. Each takes under a minute to explain and reliably gets the whole room laughing.

Gift Exchange Games With a Twist

  • White Elephant (steal-a-gift): Everyone brings one wrapped, funny, low-cost gift. Players draw numbers and pick a gift in order, but each new player may instead steal an already opened gift. Set a "stolen three times and it is frozen" rule to keep the peace. This is the single most talked-about game at adult parties.
  • Left-Right Story Game: Guests sit in a circle holding their own wrapped gift while the host reads a silly Christmas story stuffed with the words "left" and "right". Every time the word is read, gifts pass that direction. Where your gift lands when the story ends is what you keep.
  • Dice Gift Swap: Gifts sit in the centre. Roll two dice: roll a pair and you may swap your gift with anyone's; roll a seven and everyone passes left. Three rounds, then unwrap. Brilliantly chaotic for family groups.

Games for Mixed-Age Family Groups

  • Christmas Charades: Write festive prompts on slips — "wrapping a present", "the dog ate the tinsel", "Grandma tasting the gravy" — and act them out in teams. Acting without words levels every age difference.
  • Two Truths and a Lie, Christmas Edition: Each person shares two true holiday memories and one invented one. The room votes on the lie. Grandparents are shockingly good at this game.
  • Christmas Bingo: Cards filled with things likely to happen during your party: "someone mentions the weather", "a cracker joke gets groaned at", "the dog wears a hat". First to a full line wins a small prize and eternal glory.
  • Name That Carol: Play or hum the first five seconds of a Christmas song. Teams race to name it. Finish with a full-room singalong of the hardest one.

Office Party Games That Stay Professional

Work parties need games that are quick, seated, and impossible to embarrass anyone with. These three are office tested:

  • Desk Decorating Contest: Fifteen minutes, one bag of cheap decorations per team, judges from another department. Photograph the results for the team chat.
  • Festive Trivia Tournament: Short rounds on Christmas films, songs and traditions. Split departments into mixed teams so people meet someone new. Our trivia format guide shows how to run rounds and scoring.
  • Guess the Baby Photo: Everyone submits a childhood Christmas photo in advance. Match photo to colleague on a printed sheet. Sweet, safe, and surprisingly competitive.

Active Games for Kids (and Energetic Adults)

  • Candy Cane Relay: Each player holds a candy cane and must hook and pass another candy cane to the next person without hands touching the passed one. Dropped canes restart the chain. Silly, fast and very loud.
  • Reindeer Antler Ring Toss: One volunteer wears inflatable antlers (or holds two branches) while teammates toss soft rings from a marked line. Rotate the reindeer every two minutes.
  • Snowball Spoon Race: Cotton balls carried on spoons across the room without hands or blowing penalties. Cotton weighs nothing, which is exactly why it is hilarious.
  • Wrap Race: Pairs wrap an awkward object — a football, a rolling pin — against the clock. Judge on neatness at your peril.
The golden rule of party games: end each one while people are still laughing, and you will be asked to run the games again next year.

How to Run Games Without Killing the Party

Announce the plan early so nobody schedules their exit before the good part. Alternate one seated game with one active game, and never force an unwilling guest into the spotlight — scorekeepers and judges are honourable roles too. Small wrapped prizes (chocolates, candles, novelty socks) raise effort levels out of all proportion to their cost. And weave games into the wider evening: decor ideas, themes and food from our Christmas Party Ideas guide and festive recipes collection turn a good party into the one people request again.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular Christmas party game?

The White Elephant gift exchange wins at adult parties because stealing a popular gift creates instant drama. For family groups with children, Christmas charades is the most reliable all-ages choice.

How many games should I plan for a Christmas party?

Two or three games across an evening is plenty. One early icebreaker as guests arrive, one main event after food, and one quick game before people drift home.

What are good prizes for Christmas party games?

Keep prizes small and wrapped: quality chocolate, a scented candle, novelty socks, or a funny trophy that returns every year. The wrapping matters more than the value.

How do I include shy guests in party games?

Offer team games instead of solo spotlights, let people opt into roles like scorekeeper or judge, and never eliminate players publicly. Teams of mixed ages also help everyone relax.

What games work for a virtual Christmas party?

Festive trivia, Two Truths and a Lie, and a best-decorated-background contest all work perfectly over video calls with zero setup.

Conclusion

A great Christmas game is not the cleverest one in the shop; it is the one that gets your uncle, your toddler, and your boss laughing in the same minute. Pick two from this list, keep the prizes small, and let the room do the rest.
