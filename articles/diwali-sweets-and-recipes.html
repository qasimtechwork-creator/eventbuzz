Diwali Sweets & Recipes: Easy Homemade Mithai Ideas

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Nothing says Diwali like a plate of homemade mithai. The recipes below are the festival's greatest hits, simplified for real home kitchens: five sweets and two savouries with short ingredient lists, clear steps and make-ahead notes. Start a few days before Diwali on Sunday, November 8, 2026, and your sweet boxes will be ready to gift with the wishes from our Happy Diwali Wishes hub.

Quick answer: The easiest Diwali sweets for beginners are coconut ladoo (3 ingredients, no cooking beyond warming) and besan ladoo. Kaju katli, jalebi and gujiya take one careful attempt each and reward you with shop-level results.

1. Besan Ladoo — The Festival Classic

You need: 2 cups gram flour (besan), 1 cup powdered sugar, 1/2 cup melted ghee, 1/2 tsp cardamom, chopped pistachios.

  1. Roast the besan in ghee on low heat for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring constantly, until golden and nutty-smelling.
  2. Cool until just warm, then mix in sugar and cardamom.
  3. Roll into balls while still slightly warm; press a pistachio on each. Sets in 30 minutes, keeps 2 weeks airtight.

Key tip: Patience at the roasting stage is everything. Pale besan tastes raw; deep golden besan tastes like celebration.

2. Coconut Ladoo — Easiest of All

You need: 2 cups desiccated coconut, 1 cup condensed milk, 1/2 tsp cardamom, extra coconut for rolling.

  1. Warm coconut and condensed milk together in a pan for 4 to 5 minutes until the mixture leaves the sides.
  2. Stir in cardamom, cool slightly, roll into balls and coat in coconut.
  3. Chill 1 hour. Keeps a week in the fridge and disappears in a day.

3. Kaju Katli — The Gift Box Star

You need: 2 cups cashews, 1 cup sugar, 1/2 cup water, 1 tsp ghee, optional silver leaf (varak).

  1. Grind cashews to a fine powder in short pulses so they do not turn oily.
  2. Make a one-string sugar syrup, add the cashew powder and cook, stirring, until the mixture forms a soft dough that leaves the pan.
  3. Knead briefly with ghee, roll between parchment to 5mm, apply silver leaf if using, and cut into diamonds. Rest 30 minutes before boxing.

4. Jalebi — Crisp, Syrupy Spirals

You need: 1 cup flour, 2 tbsp cornflour, 1/2 tsp baking soda, 3/4 cup yogurt-water mix for batter; syrup of 1.5 cups sugar, 1 cup water, saffron and a squeeze of lemon.

  1. Whisk a flowing batter and rest it 20 minutes (or overnight for a gentle tang).
  2. Make the syrup to one-string consistency with saffron and lemon; keep it warm.
  3. Pipe spirals from a squeeze bottle into medium-hot ghee, fry until crisp and golden, then soak 30 seconds in syrup. Serve warm.

Key tip: The syrup must be warm, never boiling, when the jalebis land in it, or they go soft.

5. Gujiya — The Crescent Everyone Waits For

You need: pastry of 2 cups flour, 4 tbsp ghee, water; filling of 1 cup roasted khoya (or milk powder mix), 1/2 cup sugar, chopped nuts, coconut and cardamom.

  1. Rub ghee into flour, knead a firm dough and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Roll small circles, fill with a spoon of khoya mixture, fold into crescents and seal the edges with a fork or mould.
  3. Fry on medium-low until evenly golden. Cool fully before storing; keeps 10 days.

Savoury Balance: Two Quick Snacks

  • Namak pare: Seasoned flour diamonds, fried crisp with ajwain and black pepper. The tea-time partner every sweet plate needs.
  • Roasted masala nuts and makhana: Toss cashews, almonds or fox nuts in ghee, salt, chaat masala and a pinch of chilli; roast until crisp. Five minutes, endless snacking.

Make-Ahead Plan and Gifting

  1. Nov 2 to 3: Gujiya and namak pare (they keep longest).
  2. Nov 4 to 5: Besan ladoo and kaju katli.
  3. Nov 6 to 7: Coconut ladoo and roasted nuts; pack gift boxes with a wish card from our wishes hub.
  4. Nov 8, Diwali day: Fresh jalebi, warm and crisp, for the puja and evening guests.

Layer sweets in boxes with parchment between rows, add a small diya or dried flowers on top, and photograph your platter for the Diwali Images collection before the first box leaves the house.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Diwali sweet is easiest for a first-timer?

Coconut ladoo. Three ingredients, one pan, no syrup stages and no frying. Besan ladoo is the next step up and teaches the roasting skill behind half of all mithai.

How far ahead can I make Diwali sweets?

Gujiya and namak pare keep 10 days to 2 weeks airtight; besan ladoo about 2 weeks; kaju katli a week refrigerated; coconut ladoo a week in the fridge. Make jalebi fresh on the day.

Why did my kaju katli turn hard or sticky?

Hard means the mixture cooked too long or the syrup went past one string; sticky means it needed another minute in the pan. Aim for a soft dough that leaves the pan cleanly and kneads smooth while warm.

Can I make these sweets with less sugar?

Yes, reduce sugar by about a quarter in ladoos without hurting texture. For syrup sweets like jalebi and gujiya, keep the syrup as written, since it also preserves and crisps the sweet.

What sweets should I gift at Diwali 2026?

Kaju katli and besan ladoo travel best and please everyone. Pack them by November 7 with a handwritten wish; our Diwali wishes hub has the words ready.

Conclusion

Five sweets, two snacks and one unhurried week: that is all it takes for a fully homemade Diwali. Start with the ladoos, and by the time the diyas are lit your kitchen will smell like the festival itself.
