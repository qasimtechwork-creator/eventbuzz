Halloween in Pakistan 2026: How to Celebrate

Published October 17, 2026 · Updated October 17, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team · 4 min read

Halloween in Pakistan has grown from a curiosity into a genuine October tradition — especially among students, young professionals, and the party scenes of Karachi, Lahore, and Islamabad. It looks a little different here than in the West, and that is exactly what makes it fun.

This guide covers celebrating Halloween in Pakistan in 2026: party ideas, costume sourcing, respectful celebrating, and practical tips for the local scene.

How Pakistan Celebrates Halloween

Halloween here is primarily a party and campus phenomenon rather than a trick-or-treat one. Expect:

  • University and college parties: campuses host the biggest Halloween nights — costume contests, themed music, decorated venues.
  • Cafes and restaurants: many run Halloween-themed nights in late October with decor, costume discounts, and spooky menus.
  • Private house parties: the heart of Pakistani Halloween — friends, DIY decor, and themed food.
  • Office parties: multinational companies and startups increasingly do Halloween Fridays.
  • Mall events: some malls run kids’ costume parades and face-painting stalls.

Door-to-door trick-or-treating is rare here — the celebration centres on parties, not neighbourhood rounds.

Costumes: Where to Find Them

  • Online: Daraz and Instagram sellers stock costumes from early October — order by mid-October for delivery buffer.
  • Local markets: Ichhra and Liberty (Lahore), Tariq Road (Karachi) have costume and accessory sellers seasonally.
  • DIY: the most popular route — thrifted pieces plus creativity. Our under-$20 DIY guide works perfectly with local markets.
  • Makeup over outfits: professional-looking Halloween makeup (available at local beauty stores) transforms simple black clothes.

Weather note: late October in Pakistan is pleasant, not cold — skip heavy layered costumes. Breathable fabrics win.

Party Ideas That Work Locally

  • Desi-horror theme: churel, jinn, and horror-movie references beat imported tropes for local laughs.
  • Horror movie night: project a film, serve themed snacks, keep it cosy.
  • Costume chai party: daytime-friendly, photogenic, very Pakistani.
  • Murder mystery: works brilliantly with friend groups — see our murder mystery guide.
  • Qawwali-gone-spooky night: live music meets Halloween decor. Unforgettable.

Celebrating Respectfully

Halloween is still new enough here that thoughtfulness matters:

  • Keep it opt-in: not everyone celebrates — invite, don’t assume.
  • Respect religious sensitivities: avoid costumes mocking any faith; keep imagery playful, not blasphemous.
  • Consider the neighbours: loud late parties in residential areas cause friction — book venues or keep volumes reasonable.
  • Family-friendly options: daytime kids’ events with crafts and mild spookiness introduce the fun without the fright.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Halloween celebrated in Pakistan?

Yes, increasingly — mainly as parties among students and young adults in major cities. It is a social celebration rather than a traditional or religious one.

Do kids trick-or-treat in Pakistan?

Rarely door-to-door. Instead, malls and schools sometimes organise costume events and treat activities for children.

Where can I buy Halloween costumes in Pakistan?

Daraz, Instagram sellers, and local markets (Liberty/Icchra in Lahore, Tariq Road in Karachi) from early October. DIY from thrifted pieces is the most popular route.

What should I wear for Halloween in Pakistan’s weather?

Late October is mild — choose breathable fabrics and skip heavy layers. Makeup-heavy looks work brilliantly in this climate.

Conclusion

Halloween in Pakistan is what you make it: a campus blowout, a cosy house party, or a creative night with friends. Celebrate in your own style, respectfully — and enjoy watching the tradition grow year by year.
