Best Advent Calendars 2026: Countdown to Christmas in Style

By EventBuzz Team • October 15, 2026 • 5 min read

Few traditions build excitement like an advent calendar. Opening one small door every morning from December 1 to Christmas Eve turns an ordinary month into a daily celebration. The classic version dates back to 19th-century Germany, where families marked the days of Advent with chalk lines or lit candles. Today the choice is enormous: chocolate classics, luxury beauty boxes, toy sets and refillable calendars you can fill yourself. Here is how to pick the right one for 2026.

Classic Chocolate and Candy Calendars

The chocolate advent calendar is the version most of us grew up with, and it remains the best-selling style every year. Look for one with real chocolate rather than waxy novelty pieces, especially for kids who will notice the difference. Fair-trade and organic options are widely available now and taste noticeably better. If you want a step up from the flat cardboard box, three-dimensional designs shaped like houses, trees or trains make a lovely mantelpiece even after the doors are empty. Store the calendar away from radiators so the chocolate keeps its shape until December 25.

Beauty, Coffee and Luxury Advent Calendars

Grown-up advent calendars have become a December staple. Beauty calendars from cosmetics brands pack mini skincare, fragrance and makeup behind each door, and they are a clever way to try products before buying full sizes. Coffee and tea calendars work the same way, offering a new blend to wake up with each morning. The key is honest value: add up the mini sizes and compare them with the price before you buy. The best luxury calendars include a mix of full-size and deluxe-mini items rather than sample sachets, which rarely feel worth the money.

DIY Refillable Advent Calendars

A refillable wooden or fabric advent calendar is the most sustainable and personal option, and it pays for itself within a couple of years. Fill the drawers or pockets with anything that fits the person: chocolates, tea bags, handwritten notes, small jewelry, lottery scratch cards or tiny Lego sets for kids. Pocket-style fabric calendars hang neatly on a door and store flat in January. A practical tip is to wrap each filler in tissue paper first, so even a small item feels like a proper surprise when the drawer opens.

Advent Calendars for Kids

For children, the ritual matters more than the gift. Toy-based calendars with a new figure or building piece each day let kids assemble a small scene by Christmas Eve, which keeps the excitement growing instead of fading after week one. Activity calendars are another favorite: each door hides a slip of paper with something to do together, like baking cookies, watching a holiday movie or driving to see Christmas lights. Balance is sensible too. A door a day is already exciting, so there is no need for expensive contents behind every single one.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should you start an advent calendar?

Most calendars run from December 1 to December 24, with the final door opened on Christmas Eve. Some religious traditions start on the first Sunday of Advent instead, which falls in late November, so check your calendar's door count before you begin.

What can I put inside a DIY advent calendar?

Anything small and personal: chocolates, tea, notes, stickers, hair accessories, small toys or coins. Mix edible treats with little experiences, like a coupon for a movie night, to keep every day interesting.

Are luxury advent calendars worth the money?

They can be, if the contents add up to more than the price. Check reviews that list what is actually inside, and compare the total value of the products against buying your favorites individually. Calendars full of sample sizes rarely beat that test.

What age are advent calendars appropriate for?

There is no fixed age. Toddlers love picture or chocolate calendars with help from a parent, school-age kids enjoy toy and activity versions, and adults have their own luxury options. Just keep small parts away from very young children.
