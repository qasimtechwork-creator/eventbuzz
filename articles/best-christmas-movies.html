Best Christmas Movies to Watch With Family This Year

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

Some films are just background noise, and some films become part of the furniture of Christmas itself. The titles below have earned their place on family watchlists across generations: warm stories, gentle humour, and endings that leave everyone in the room a little kinder than they were ninety minutes earlier. Pick by mood, not by ranking, and let the youngest person in the room choose at least once.

Quick answer: For a guaranteed all-ages win, start with Home Alone for laughs, The Polar Express for younger kids, It's a Wonderful Life for the grown-ups, and Klaus for a modern classic everyone ends up loving.

Timeless Classics the Whole Family Can Share

These have been watched, quoted, and rewatched for decades because the emotions in them never date.

  • It's a Wonderful Life (1946) — The original Christmas tear-jerker. A small-town man who feels he has failed discovers, through a gentle guardian angel, how many lives his kindness quietly held together. Best for older kids and adults; have tissues nearby.
  • A Christmas Carol — Dickens' story of Ebenezer Scrooge works in almost every version. The 1951 adaptation with Alastair Sim is the purest, while The Muppet Christmas Carol (1992) delivers the same redemption arc with songs, jokes, and zero nightmares for small children.
  • Miracle on 34th Street (1947) — A kind old man who claims to be Santa Claus ends up proving his identity in a New York courtroom. Sweet, clever, and perfect for families who like their Christmas magic with a little logic puzzle attached.
  • White Christmas (1954) — Bing Crosby, snowy Vermont, big musical numbers, and the most famous Christmas song ever written. Ideal for grandparents and anyone who believes a finale should involve the whole inn singing.

Animated Favourites for Younger Kids

Short attention spans welcome. These run short, look beautiful, and carry their message lightly.

  • The Polar Express (2004) — A midnight train ride to the North Pole for a boy who is starting to doubt. The "believe" theme lands with children and quietly ambushes adults.
  • Klaus (2019) — A hand-drawn stunner about a spoiled postman and a reclusive toymaker who accidentally invent the Santa legend together. Many families now rank it beside the classics.
  • How the Grinch Stole Christmas (1966) — The original half-hour cartoon, narrated by Boris Karloff. The Grinch's heart grows three sizes and so does everyone's in the room. The 2018 animated feature is a fine longer alternative.
  • A Charlie Brown Christmas (1965) — Twenty-five quiet minutes about one small tree and what the season is actually for. Linus's speech still silences a room.
  • Arthur Christmas (2011) — Santa's clumsy youngest son races to deliver one forgotten present before dawn. Fast, funny, and genuinely touching at the end.

Comedy Picks for Laughs All Round

When the day has been long and the room needs loosening up, these deliver.

  • Home Alone (1990) — Eight-year-old Kevin, accidentally left behind at Christmas, defends the house with legendary traps. The slapstick thrills kids; the final ten minutes melt parents every single time. Home Alone 2: Lost in New York makes a natural double bill.
  • Elf (2003) — Will Ferrell as a human raised by elves brings pure, unfiltered joy to cynical New York. Endlessly quotable and safe for nearly every age.
  • The Santa Clause (1994) — Tim Allen accidentally inherits the Santa job, beard, belly and all. A warm father-and-son story underneath the jokes.
  • National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation (1989) — Clark Griswold's quest for the perfect family Christmas collapses in glorious stages. Best for teens and adults; the light-display scene alone earns its annual screening.

Cozy Picks for a Quiet Night In

  • The Holiday (2006) — Two women swap homes across the Atlantic for Christmas and slowly get their lives back. Gentle romance, beautiful cottages, zero stress. For adults and older teens.
  • Little Women (2019 or 1994) — Not strictly a Christmas film, but the March sisters' snowy holidays make it feel like one. A beautiful choice for mothers, daughters, and book lovers.
  • The Chronicles of Narnia: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe (2005) — A land where it is always winter but never Christmas, until it finally is. Father Christmas's arrival mid-film is a genuine thrill for kids.
Movie-night rule that never fails: the person who makes the popcorn holds the remote, and nobody is allowed to call a film "slow" until the cocoa has gone cold.

How to Run the Perfect Christmas Movie Night

Dim the lamps and let the tree do the lighting. Put phones in a basket by the door and trade them for blankets. Serve one savoury snack and one sweet one, and time the film so hot drinks arrive at the midpoint. If children span wide ages, screen the animated pick first and the classic after the little ones are asleep. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular Christmas movie of all time?

Home Alone and It's a Wonderful Life trade the top spot depending on the poll. Home Alone leads with younger audiences for its comedy, while It's a Wonderful Life remains the most beloved classic among adults.

Which Christmas movie is best for very young children?

The 1966 Grinch cartoon and A Charlie Brown Christmas are both under half an hour, gentle in tone, and made exactly for small attention spans.

Are any of these movies scary for kids?

Everything listed here is family friendly. The Muppet Christmas Carol keeps Dickens' ghost story playful, and younger children may prefer Klaus or Arthur Christmas to the slightly spookier moments in The Polar Express's puppet scenes.

Where can I watch these Christmas movies?

Availability changes every season across streaming services, TV schedules and rental stores, so search the title on your usual service before movie night. Most of these classics are widely carried each December.

What snacks go best with a Christmas movie night?

Popcorn with a little cinnamon sugar, ginger biscuits, hot chocolate with marshmallows, and a bowl of clementines keep hands busy without a sugar crash before bedtime.

Conclusion

The best Christmas movie is the one your family quotes on December 27th. Rotate who chooses, keep the cocoa coming, and let at least one old favourite return every year. Merry watching from all of us at EventBuzz.
