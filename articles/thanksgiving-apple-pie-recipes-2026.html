Thanksgiving Apple Pie 2026: Flaky Crust Recipes Guests Love

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

Nothing says Thanksgiving like the smell of apple pie in the oven. For the November 26, 2026 holiday table, a great apple pie is the dessert guests remember long after the turkey is gone: buttery, shattering crust, cinnamon-spiced filling, and slices that hold together on the plate. The good news is that a bakery-worthy pie is entirely about technique, not talent. Pick the right apples, keep your crust cold, and follow the timing below, and your pie will be the one people ask about next year.

Pick the Right Apples: Mix, Do Not Match

The single biggest upgrade to your apple pie is using two or three apple varieties instead of one. A single-variety pie tastes flat, because each apple brings only one note. Granny Smith gives bright tartness and holds its shape. Honeycrisp adds honeyed sweetness and juicy crunch. Braeburn or Golden Delicious melt into a soft, jammy background that binds the filling. For one 9-inch pie you need about 3 to 3.5 pounds of apples, roughly 7 to 8 medium ones. Try 3 Granny Smith, 2 Honeycrisp, and 2 Golden Delicious for a filling that is tart, sweet, and complex at once.

Slice the apples a consistent quarter inch thick so they cook evenly, and toss them immediately with a tablespoon of lemon juice. The acid slows browning and brightens the spice flavor. Do not skip this step if you slice ahead; apples begin oxidizing within minutes.

The Flaky Crust Formula

Flakiness comes from cold butter and minimal handling. Whisk together 2 and 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, 1 teaspoon sugar, and 1/2 teaspoon fine salt. Cut in 1 cup (2 sticks) of very cold unsalted butter, diced into half-inch cubes, using a pastry blender or your fingertips until the mixture looks like coarse crumbs with pea-sized butter pieces still visible. Those visible pieces are the whole secret: they melt in the oven and create the separate, shattery layers.

Add ice water one tablespoon at a time, 4 to 6 tablespoons total, just until the dough holds together when squeezed. Divide into two discs, wrap, and chill at least 1 hour or overnight. Roll each disc on a lightly floured surface into a 12-inch circle, turning a quarter turn between rolls to keep it round. If the butter starts to feel soft or greasy, stop and chill everything for 15 minutes. Cold dough is non-negotiable.

Recipe 1: Classic Double-Crust Apple Pie

This is the benchmark Thanksgiving pie. Line a 9-inch pie plate with the bottom crust, letting the excess hang over the edge. Toss your sliced apples with 3/4 cup granulated sugar, 2 tablespoons flour, 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon, 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg, a pinch of salt, and 1 tablespoon lemon juice. Mound the filling high in the crust; it shrinks as it bakes. Dot with 1 tablespoon of cold butter cut into small pieces, then lay on the top crust, trim to a 1-inch overhang, fold under, and crimp with your fingers or a fork. Cut 4 vents in the top.

Bake at 425°F for 20 minutes, then reduce to 375°F and bake 35 to 40 minutes more, until the crust is deep golden and the filling bubbles through the vents. The bubbling is your doneness signal: it means the filling has reached about 195°F and the starches have thickened. Cool on a rack for at least 3 hours before slicing so the filling sets. Cutting warm gives you soup; patience gives you clean wedges.

Recipe 2: Salted Caramel Apple Pie

For a pie with extra wow factor, drizzle half a cup of salted caramel sauce over the apples before adding the top crust, then finish the baked, cooled pie with another drizzle and a sprinkle of flaky sea salt. Use a lattice top for this one so the caramel peeks through. Reduce the sugar in the filling to 1/2 cup to compensate. The salt is doing real work here: it cuts the caramel's sweetness and makes the apple flavor taste more like itself. Warm the caramel slightly before drizzling so it flows instead of clumping.

Weave a Lattice Like a Pro

A lattice looks impressive but is just a pattern. Follow these steps:

  • Roll the top crust into a 12-inch circle and cut 10 strips, each 3/4 inch wide, with a pizza wheel.
  • Lay 5 strips vertically across the filled pie, spaced evenly.
  • Fold back strips 2 and 4, lay one horizontal strip across, and unfold them.
  • Fold back strips 1, 3, and 5, lay the next horizontal strip, and unfold. Repeat down the pie.
  • Trim the overhang, crimp the edge, and brush with an egg wash of 1 beaten egg plus 1 tablespoon milk. Sprinkle with coarse sugar.

Egg wash is what gives the crust its glossy, deep-golden finish, and the coarse sugar adds sparkle and crunch. If the edges brown too fast, cover them with strips of foil after 20 minutes.

Make-Ahead and Storage

Apple pie is one of the most Thanksgiving-friendly desserts because it keeps beautifully. Bake it the day before, cool completely, and store at room temperature under a loose tent of foil or a cake dome. It will taste just as good on Thursday. For longer planning, assemble the pie unbaked, wrap tightly in plastic and foil, and freeze up to 3 months; bake straight from frozen, adding 15 to 20 minutes to the time. Reheat slices at 300°F for 10 to 15 minutes to re-crisp the crust. Serve warm with vanilla ice cream or a slice of sharp cheddar, the classic New England way.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best apples for Thanksgiving apple pie?

A mix of tart and sweet: Granny Smith for structure and tartness, Honeycrisp for sweetness, and Golden Delicious for a jammy background. Three varieties beat one every time.

How do I stop my apple pie from getting a soggy bottom?

Bake on a preheated baking sheet or pizza stone so the bottom crust sets fast, and start the bake at 425°F before reducing the heat. Tossing the apples with 2 tablespoons of flour also helps thicken the juices.

Can I make apple pie the day before Thanksgiving?

Yes. Bake it fully on Wednesday, cool completely, and keep it at room temperature under foil. Reheat slices at 300°F for 10 to 15 minutes before serving.

How do I keep the crust edges from burning?

Cover the rim with strips of aluminum foil or a pie shield after the first 20 minutes of baking, once the edges are golden.

Should apple pie be served warm or cold?

Slightly warm is ideal, with a scoop of vanilla ice cream. Let the pie cool 3 hours after baking so the filling sets, then warm individual slices.
