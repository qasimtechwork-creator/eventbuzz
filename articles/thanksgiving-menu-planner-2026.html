Thanksgiving Menu Planner 2026: Plan Your Perfect Holiday Feast

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

A great Thanksgiving dinner is planned, not improvised. This menu planner walks you through the decisions in the right order: headcount first, then the menu skeleton, then portions, then the make-ahead schedule. Follow it once and you will wonder why the holiday ever felt chaotic.

Step 1: Nail the Headcount

Everything flows from the number of seats. Confirm RSVPs two weeks out, and count the maybes as yeses — Thanksgiving has a way of adding people. Ask about dietary needs now, not at the table: vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free, nut allergies. One question in the group text saves you from a panicked side dish on Thursday.

Write down the number and tape it to the fridge. Every portion calculation below starts here.

The Balanced Menu Template

A classic Thanksgiving menu has seven parts. You do not need all seven, but check each one off — or consciously skip it — so nothing is forgotten:

  • The bird: turkey, or a vegetarian centerpiece like a stuffed squash
  • The starch: mashed potatoes, stuffing, or sweet potatoes — pick two
  • The green: green bean casserole, Brussels sprouts, or a simple salad
  • The sweet-tart: cranberry sauce, homemade or canned (no judgment)
  • The bread: dinner rolls, cornbread, or biscuits
  • The gravy: yes, it gets its own line — it is that important
  • The dessert: pie, obviously — one per eight guests is the rule

Portion Math That Actually Works

The biggest menu mistake is cooking for the wrong number. Use these per-person guides for a dinner with leftovers (because leftovers are the point):

  • Turkey: 1 to 1.5 pounds per person (whole bird weight)
  • Mashed potatoes: about 3/4 cup per person
  • Stuffing: about 3/4 cup per person
  • Vegetables and sides: 1/2 cup per person per side
  • Gravy: 1/3 cup per person (double it — gravy always runs out)
  • Cranberry sauce: 1/4 cup per person
  • Pie: one 9-inch pie serves 8
  • Rolls: 2 per person
  • Drinks: about 3 drinks per guest for a 4-hour evening

Handling Dietary Needs Gracefully

You do not need a separate menu — you need smart swaps. Roast vegetables in olive oil instead of butter so vegans can eat them. Make the stuffing vegetarian and serve sausage on the side. Put nuts on the salad as a topping, not mixed in. Label the dishes with little cards so guests with restrictions can navigate without interrogating you mid-dinner.

For the main event, a stuffed acorn squash or a mushroom Wellington gives vegetarian guests a centerpiece instead of a plate of sides. It takes one extra dish, and it says you thought of them.

The Make-Ahead Map

The secret to a calm Thanksgiving is that half the menu is finished before Thursday. Cranberry sauce, pie dough, and chopped vegetables can be done days ahead. Pies bake beautifully on Wednesday. Casseroles assemble the day before and bake day-of. The only things that truly demand Thursday are the turkey, the gravy, and anything that must be served crisp.

Write each dish on a sticky note with its oven temperature and time, and stick them on the fridge in cooking order. On Thanksgiving morning you will have a plan instead of a panic.

Assigning Dishes to Guests

If guests offer to bring something — and they will — say yes with direction. Keep the turkey, gravy and one signature side for yourself, and hand out the rest by category: appetizers to the early arrivers, a vegetable side to the confident cook, dessert to the baker, drinks and ice to whoever asked "what can I bring" with panic in their voice.

Write assignments down and text confirmations the Monday before. Vague plans produce duplicate dishes; a short text — "You're on green bean casserole, we're so excited" — produces exactly what the menu needs. Always keep one emergency side in your own plan in case someone cancels.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many side dishes should I serve?

Three to five sides is the sweet spot for most tables — enough variety without a cooking marathon.

How much turkey do I need per person?

One to one and a half pounds of whole bird per person, which leaves room for leftovers.

What can I make ahead for Thanksgiving?

Cranberry sauce, pie dough, chopped vegetables, casseroles (assembled), and most desserts — all one to three days ahead.

How do I plan a menu with vegetarians?

Keep sides vegetarian-friendly, make stuffing meat-free with sausage on the side, and add one showpiece like a stuffed squash.

How many pies do I need?

One nine-inch pie per eight guests, in at least two varieties — pumpkin plus one wildcard like apple or pecan.
