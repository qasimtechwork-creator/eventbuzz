Hanukkah 2026: Dates, Traditions and Celebration Guide

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

Hanukkah, the Jewish Festival of Lights, is eight days of candle lighting, fried foods, games, and gathering with the people you love. Whether you grew up lighting the menorah every year or are celebrating for the first time, this guide covers everything: the exact 2026 dates, the story behind the holiday, how to light the menorah correctly, and ideas to make all eight nights feel special.

When Is Hanukkah 2026?

Hanukkah 2026 begins at sundown on Friday, December 4, 2026, when the first candle of the menorah is lit. The holiday continues through all eight nights and ends at sundown on Saturday, December 12, 2026. Because Hanukkah follows the Hebrew calendar, its dates shift on the Gregorian calendar every year, always falling somewhere between late November and late December.

Each evening during the holiday, one additional candle is lit, so by the eighth night the menorah glows with all nine flames: the eight branches plus the shamash, the helper candle used to light the others. The candle lighting should ideally happen as soon as it gets dark, when the family can gather together around the menorah, which is traditionally placed in a window where the light can be seen from the street.

The Story Behind the Festival of Lights

Hanukkah commemorates an event from the second century BCE, when a small band of Jewish fighters, the Maccabees, recaptured the Holy Temple in Jerusalem from the Seleucid king Antiochus IV, who had outlawed Jewish worship. When they rededicated the Temple, they found only enough pure olive oil to keep the eternal flame burning for one day, yet the flame miraculously burned for eight days, long enough to prepare more oil.

This is why Hanukkah lasts eight nights and why oil plays such a central role in the celebration, from the candles in the menorah to the fried foods on the table. The holiday is about resilience, faith, and the refusal to let a light go out, themes that give every candle lighting a quiet emotional weight beyond the festivities.

How to Light the Menorah

The menorah, more precisely called a hanukkiah, has nine branches: eight in a row plus the shamash, which usually sits higher or apart from the rest. On the first night, place one candle in the rightmost branch (plus the shamash). Each night, add one candle to the left, so by night eight the candles fill the menorah from right to left. You light them from left to right, newest candle first, using the shamash.

Before lighting, two blessings are recited on the first night (three, actually: a third blessing, the Shehecheyanu, is said only on night one). The candles should burn for at least 30 minutes after nightfall. One practical tip: use a drip tray or aluminum foil under the menorah, and light the shamash first, then use it to kindle each night's candles. Let the candles burn down naturally rather than blowing them out early.

Classic Hanukkah Traditions

Beyond the menorah, Hanukkah is packed with traditions that make the eight nights feel distinct:

  • Dreidel: The four-sided spinning top with Hebrew letters (nun, gimel, hey, shin) standing for "a great miracle happened there." Players spin and win or lose tokens, traditionally chocolate coins called gelt.
  • Gelt: Chocolate coins wrapped in gold foil, given to children and used as dreidel stakes. Real coins are sometimes given to older kids.
  • Fried foods: Latkes (crispy potato pancakes) and sufganiyot (jelly-filled doughnuts) celebrate the miracle of the oil.
  • Gifts: Many families give small gifts each night, often one bigger gift on a chosen night.
  • Songs: Classics like "Maoz Tzur" (Rock of Ages) and "Dreidel, Dreidel, Dreidel" are sung after the candles are lit.

The dreidel game deserves a try even if you have never played: everyone puts one token in the pot, spins in turn, and nun means nothing happens, gimel takes the whole pot, hey takes half, shin puts one in. It is simple, loud, and perfect with kids.

Modern Ways to Celebrate All Eight Nights

Many families now give each night a theme to keep the celebration fresh: a game night, a cooking night where everyone helps fry latkes, a charity night for donating toys or food, a movie night, and a night for a big family dinner. This works especially well with kids, who get something to look forward to each evening.

Host a Hanukkah party on the weekend night in the middle of the holiday, when most guests can come after dark for the candle lighting. Set up a latke bar with toppings, a dreidel tournament with a small prize, and a hot chocolate station. Decorate in blue, silver, and white with Stars of David, string lights, and a beautiful menorah as the centerpiece. Eight nights is a long celebration, so pace yourself: not every night needs to be an event. Some of the best Hanukkah memories are just the family quietly watching the candles burn.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does Hanukkah start in 2026?

Hanukkah 2026 begins at sundown on Friday, December 4, 2026, with the lighting of the first candle, and ends at sundown on Saturday, December 12, 2026.

How many candles are lit on the menorah?

Eight candles, one for each night, plus a ninth helper candle called the shamash used to light the others. Add one candle per night from right to left, and light them from left to right.

What foods are traditional for Hanukkah?

Foods fried in oil, especially latkes (potato pancakes) and sufganiyot (jelly doughnuts), to honor the miracle of the Temple oil that burned for eight days.

What is the dreidel game?

A spinning-top game where players spin a four-sided dreidel marked with Hebrew letters and win or lose tokens like chocolate gelt. Nun means do nothing, gimel takes the pot, hey takes half, shin adds a token.

Do you give gifts every night of Hanukkah?

Many families do give a small gift each of the eight nights, but it is not a religious requirement. Some give one larger gift on a single night, or focus on gelt and experiences instead.
