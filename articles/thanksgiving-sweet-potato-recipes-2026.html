12 Sweet Potato Recipes for Thanksgiving 2026: Casseroles, Pies & More

By EventBuzz Team • October 16, 2026 • 5 min read

Sweet potatoes are Thanksgiving's most versatile player. They swing from gooey marshmallow casserole to elegant savory gratin to silky pie without missing a beat, and their natural sweetness means they need less added sugar than you think. Whether your family demands the classic casserole or you want to branch out this year, these twelve recipes cover every way to love this holiday star.

The Marshmallow Casserole, Perfected

The iconic casserole works because of contrast: creamy sweet potato base, crunchy pecan streusel, and a torched marshmallow cap. Start by roasting 3 pounds of sweet potatoes whole at 400°F for 50 to 60 minutes until completely soft; roasted beats boiled for flavor every time. Scoop out the flesh and mash with half a cup of brown sugar, a quarter cup of melted butter, a splash of cream, vanilla, cinnamon, and a pinch of salt. Spread in a buttered 9x13 dish.

For the streusel, mix a cup of chopped pecans with half a cup each of flour and brown sugar and a quarter cup of cold butter, rubbed to crumbs. Here is the pro move: bake the casserole with the streusel for 25 minutes first, then add a layer of mini marshmallows and broil 2 to 3 minutes until golden. Baking the marshmallows from the start turns them into a rubbery sheet; the late addition gives you distinct toasted puffs over the crunchy pecans. It is the version that converts skeptics.

Savory Takes That Steal the Show

Maple-roasted sweet potatoes are the simplest upgrade: cut into 1-inch cubes, toss with olive oil, maple syrup, salt, and fresh thyme, and roast at 425°F for 30 to 35 minutes until caramelized at the edges. Whipped sweet potatoes with brown butter and a pinch of cayenne give you a savory mash that belongs next to turkey just as naturally as the white potato version. For something elegant, layer thinly sliced sweet potatoes with gruyere and cream in a gratin dish; 45 minutes at 375°F yields a golden, bubbling showpiece.

Hasselback sweet potatoes look stunning with almost no effort: slice thin slits across each potato without cutting through, brush with garlic butter, and roast until the edges crisp. Twice-baked sweet potatoes scoop the roasted flesh, mix it with cream cheese and chives, and refill the skins for individual portions that reheat perfectly. And sweet potato biscuits, made with roasted sweet potato in the dough, are flaky, orange-flecked, and unbeatable warm with honey butter.

Sweet Potato Pie vs. Pumpkin Pie

Sweet potato pie is the Southern answer to pumpkin, and many tasters prefer it. The flavor is deeper and less squashy, with a velvety texture that canned pumpkin cannot match. Roast the sweet potatoes rather than boiling them to concentrate their flavor and avoid a watery filling. Blend the flesh with evaporated milk, eggs, brown sugar, butter, vanilla, and warm spices until completely smooth; any fibers will show in the slice.

Blind-bake the crust for 10 minutes with pie weights before filling to guarantee a crisp bottom. Bake at 350°F for 50 to 60 minutes until the edges are set but the center still jiggles slightly; it firms up as it cools. Serve with barely sweetened whipped cream and a dusting of cinnamon. Make it a day ahead; like pumpkin pie, it slices cleaner after a night in the fridge.

Make-Ahead Master Plan

Sweet potato dishes are make-ahead friendly across the board. Follow this timeline to keep Thanksgiving day calm:

  • 3 days ahead: roast sweet potatoes for casseroles and pies; refrigerate the scooped flesh in airtight containers.
  • 2 days ahead: assemble the marshmallow casserole (without marshmallows) or the gratin; refrigerate unbaked.
  • 1 day ahead: bake sweet potato pie and sweet potato biscuits; both keep beautifully overnight.
  • Thanksgiving morning: roast cubes and hasselbacks fresh; they only need 30 to 35 minutes and taste best hot.
  • Reheating: warm casseroles covered at 350°F for 25 minutes, then add marshmallows and broil. Never microwave a gratin; the oven keeps the top crisp.

Picking and Storing Sweet Potatoes

Choose firm sweet potatoes with smooth, unblemished skin and no soft spots; smaller ones tend to be sweeter and roast more evenly. Despite the names used in American grocery stores, true yams are a different species rarely sold in the US; what is labeled yam is just a moist-fleshed sweet potato variety. Store them in a cool, dark, ventilated spot for 3 to 4 weeks, never in the refrigerator, where cold temperatures create hard centers and off flavors. Buy them two weeks before Thanksgiving without worry.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are sweet potatoes and yams the same thing?

In US grocery stores, yes, effectively. True yams are a different species rarely sold here; anything labeled yam is a moist-fleshed sweet potato variety. Use them interchangeably.

Can I use canned sweet potatoes for casserole?

You can, but roasted fresh sweet potatoes taste significantly better and are barely more work. If using canned, drain them very well and reduce added sugar, since canned versions are packed in syrup.

How do I keep sweet potato casserole from being too sweet?

Cut the sugar in the base by a third, since sweet potatoes are naturally sweet, and balance with salt and a squeeze of orange juice. The marshmallow topping adds plenty of sweetness on its own.

Can I make sweet potato casserole ahead?

Yes. Assemble it fully 2 days ahead without the marshmallows, refrigerate, and bake on Thanksgiving. Add marshmallows in the last few minutes under the broiler.

Sweet potato pie or pumpkin pie: which should I make?

Make both if you can; they are both beloved. Sweet potato pie has a deeper, less squashy flavor. If forced to choose one, pick the one your family grew up with; pie loyalty runs deep.
