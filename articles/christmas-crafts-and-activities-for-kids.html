Christmas Crafts & Activities for Kids: Easy DIY Fun at Home

Published October 10, 2026 · Updated October 10, 2026 · By EventBuzz Team

The run-up to Christmas is long for children and short for everyone else. A small stock of simple crafts solves both problems: kids get afternoons that feel like pure holiday, and you get decorations and cards made with actual love rather than money. Every idea below uses cheap, common supplies, finishes in under an hour, and produces something worth keeping on the tree year after year.

Quick answer: The three unbeatable starters are paper snowflakes for windows, salt dough ornaments for the tree, and fingerprint cards for grandparents. Total starter kit cost: paper, flour, salt, paint and glue.

Paper Snowflakes for the Windows

Fold a square of white paper into a triangle twice, then snip small triangles, diamonds and notches along the edges. Unfold slowly — the gasp is the point. Younger children can tear rather than cut for softer, fluffier flakes. Stick finished snowflakes to windows with a dab of reusable adhesive and let late-afternoon light do the decorating. Make one giant flake together for the middle of the window; it becomes a family portrait in paper.

Salt Dough Ornaments That Last for Years

Mix 2 cups plain flour, 1 cup fine salt and about 1 cup warm water into a smooth dough. Roll to roughly 5mm thick, cut stars, trees and hearts with biscuit cutters, and poke a hole near the top with a straw before baking. Bake low and slow at 100°C (210°F) for 2 to 3 hours until completely hard. Cool fully, then paint with acrylics and finish with a ribbon. Write the child's name and year on the back in marker: future-you will be very grateful. Grown-up hands should handle the oven stages; everything else belongs to the kids.

Handmade Cards People Actually Keep

  • Fingerprint reindeer: Brown thumbprints become the head; add antlers, a red nose and wobbly legs with a fine pen. Charming at age three, still charming framed at age thirty.
  • Paper strip trees: Glue green paper strips in shrinking lengths onto card to form a tree; finish with a star sticker and a glitter trunk.
  • Button snowmen: Three mismatched buttons in a column, a drawn scarf and hat, and the card is done. Perfect for using the odd buttons every drawer collects.

For what to write inside, borrow lines from our Christmas card writing guide and let children copy or dictate the words themselves.

Kitchen Crafts: Baking That Doubles as Decorating

Ginger biscuits with white icing pens turn an afternoon into an edible craft session. Bake simple shapes using the biscuit method in our Christmas recipes guide, then let children pipe snowflakes, faces and extremely ambitious reindeer. Add a ribbon hole before baking and some biscuits can hang on the tree for a day or two before being eaten, as tradition demands.

Screen-Free Activities for Long Evenings

  • Christmas story chain: One person writes a sentence beginning "On Christmas Eve, the cat knocked over…", folds the paper, and passes it on. Reading the finished nonsense aloud becomes an annual ceremony.
  • Indoor treasure hunt: Hide ten paper stars around the house with a small clue on each. The final star guards a hot chocolate upgrade: marshmallows.
  • Build a blanket fort cinema: Cushions, blankets, fairy lights if you have them, then one pick from our family Christmas movies list. Popcorn is mandatory; tickets drawn by children are adorable.
  • Kindness calendar: Instead of chocolate, each December day holds one small good deed written by the family together: feed the birds, thank the postman, donate a toy.
The craft is never really the point. Flour on sleeves, glitter in improbable places, and one wonky paper star on the window are what December actually looks like in a happy house.

Setting Up Without Losing Your Mind

Cover the table with an old cloth or newspaper before anything opens. Put supplies in the middle and finished work on a "drying tray" so masterpieces are not stacked wet. Child-safe scissors for under-sevens, and one adult-owned pair of good scissors for the tricky folds. Photograph everything at the end of the session: the tree fills up, but the photos keep the three-year-old versions forever.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Christmas crafts are best for toddlers?

Fingerprint cards, tearing-paper snowflakes, and sticking pre-cut shapes onto card are ideal. Keep sessions to 15 or 20 minutes and expect the glue to be the main attraction.

How do I make salt dough ornaments last?

Bake them until fully hard all the way through, cool completely before painting, and seal with a clear craft varnish or PVA glue coat. Store wrapped in tissue in a dry box and they last for many years.

What supplies should I buy for Christmas crafting?

Coloured card, white paper, child-safe scissors, glue sticks, washable paint or acrylics for older children, ribbon, glitter if you are brave, and biscuit cutters that double for baking.

How can crafts become Christmas gifts?

Salt dough ornaments, decorated biscuits and handmade cards make perfect grandparent gifts. Add the child's name and the year, and wrap in plain paper they decorated themselves.

How do I keep craft time calm with several children?

Give everyone the same starting materials, set a visible timer for tidy-up songs, and display every finished piece without ranking them. A drying tray and aprons prevent most disasters before they happen.

Conclusion

Children will not remember whether the snowflake was symmetrical. They will remember an afternoon when the whole table was covered in paper and nobody was in a hurry. Stock the basics, pick one craft, and let December begin.
